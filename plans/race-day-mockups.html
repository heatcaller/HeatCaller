Race Day Rethink

The Race Day page is the screen a race director looks at between races, standing up, with drivers at the stand and a marshal asking a question. Below: what goes wrong on today's page under that pressure, the rules a redesign should follow, four mockups that each try a different idea, and a build plan for the recommended one. What the screenshot shows

Today's page asks you to read sentences

Everything on the page is accurate, and it is all drawn about the same size and colour. The worst news on it (the night ends four hours late) is the quietest text on it.

    The biggest problem is in muted body text."Finishes about 9:01 PM - 4 h 02 min past the 5:00 PM out-by time" sits mid-sentence in grey, after the race count. The three ways to fix it are in the same sentence with no button.
  • 2
    The page says "start" and "not ready" at once.Start Q1 Heat 1 is the loudest button on screen while Ready to Race says the timer isn't connected. Nothing links the two, so the director finds out at the green flag.
  • 3
    "Q1 Heat 1" means four different races.The headline, the Next Up tile and three grid rows all say Q1 Heat 1. Only the small detail line says it's Stock. The grid opens on races already recorded, so the next race isn't in view.
  • 4
    Numbers without a judgement."19 drivers in" and "6 checked in" sit side by side as equals, but 13 people not checked in is a problem. "5 of 6 ready" counts setup steps and uses the same words as the drivers.
  • 5
    Too many buttons that look the same.Nine buttons, two of them pink primaries (Start Q1 Heat 1, Start Selected). The rest of the commands are on a right-click menu that you have to know about.
  • 6
    Hard to read from a few steps away.The section labels are widely spaced small caps (T H E  D A Y), there's no clock on the page, and the hidden "About" (expected time) column means you can't see when each race should go.

What a director needs between two races

In about 90 seconds: what's next (which race, which class, who's in it), can I start it (timer, drivers, marshals), am I on time (and, if not, what I can cut), and what's going wrong. Everything else (standings, glossary, editing drivers) can be one step further away.

Already in the app

ProgramNextStep works out the next race. NightPlan.For projects the finish and FinalsCuts works out the savings. NightPlan.AboutOf gives each race an expected time. MissionControl.Assess sorts every check into a good, watch or problem level. ReadyToRace knows what's left to set up. The redesign is mostly about how these are shown. Very little new logic is needed.

Rules for the redesign

What a high-pressure screen has to do

These come from places where mistakes are costly and people are rushed: aircraft cockpits, control rooms, broadcast galleries, ICU monitors. Every mockup is checked against them.

One obvious next action

One primary button on the screen, always for the next race. Every other button is secondary or behind a menu.

Hick's law · reduce choices

Quiet when things are fine

Green checks are small and dim. Only problems get size, colour and position. If everything is loud, the director stops noticing any of it.

"dark cockpit" philosophy

Never colour alone

Every status is a shape and a word as well as a colour (✓ OK, ▲ Watch, ■ Stop), so it still works for colour-blind directors, in sunlight and on a cheap projector.

WCAG 1.4.1 · redundant coding

Things stay in fixed places

The timer status is always in the same spot, and so is the Start button. A button never moves because the text above it got longer. People find things by remembering where they are.

spatial memory · no layout shift

Show the decision, not the data

Show "47 min late, and here are 3 ways to fix it" as buttons, not a sentence to work out. Times are clock times ("8:12 PM"), not durations you have to add up.

recognition over recall

Readable from a few steps away

The next race, the clock and the alarm are 28px or larger. No long words in widely spaced capitals. Contrast is at least 4.5:1, and 7:1 for anything you act on.

glanceability · WCAG 1.4.6

Mistakes can be undone

Starting a race can be undone in one step (the app's undo already covers it). Risky choices like "start anyway" need a held press, not a pop-up asking "Are you sure?" that people click through without reading.

forgiving design · avoid confirm fatigue

Easy to pick up after a distraction

A marshal pulls you away for two minutes. When you look back, the screen shows what changed while you were away and where you were, with no hunting.

situation awareness, level 1-3

Works without a mouse

Space starts the next race, H holds and U undoes, and each shortcut is printed on its button. A director holding a radio has one hand free.

keyboard shortcuts · Fitts's law

Strengths

The smallest change from today's page, because it's the same three parts in a better order. It covers every rule above. The program grid isn't lost; it opens in a drawer from "Full program ▸".

Costs

The director can no longer see all 12 races at a glance on the page. Building and editing heats becomes a drawer visit. Clubs that rebuild the program mid-night will notice.

Strengths

The best answer to "are we on time?", and the most honest picture of a late night. It suits clubs with a hard out-by time (rented halls, school gyms).

Costs

It has the most reading of the four (eleven rows). It's weak on blockers. It's a new control, because the timeline can't be built from the existing grid. It needs AboutOf to be trustworthy for every row.

  1. 1Six slots that never move or reorder. Each slot stays put whatever its state, so after a week the director glances top-left for the timer without thinking. It's driven by MissionControl.Assess levels.
  2. 2The Start button checks the lights. While anything is red, a single click doesn't start the race; a held press (a filling bar) does. No pop-up to click through.

Strengths

The best at spotting problems at a glance and at recovering from distractions. It shows driver-level detail where today's page shows only counts. The light row could also go on Race Control, so both pages read the same way.

Costs

It looks dense, and a first-time director may feel it's a lot. Two red lights look alarming even on a casual practice night, so each club needs a "this check doesn't apply tonight" switch.

Strengths

Costs

It's slow for a regular who knows the night by heart (the checklist every race gets old). A held "start anyway" every race at a no-timer club is annoying, so it needs a per-club setting for which checks to skip. It's the biggest structural change: the tabs go.

What it keeps from each

From B: the timeline, the out-by line, the preview of a fix before you apply it, crossed-out cut races. From A: one large Start card, the blocker above Start, the checks, the trimmed footer. Nothing moves to a drawer: the program is on the page, as it is today.

What it costs

The Now card sits in the sheet, so it moves down the page as races are recorded. To keep the Start button from moving, done races fold away and the sheet scrolls to keep the "now" line at the same height. It's more work than A alone, because the program grid gets a custom row for the next race.

Build plan

A, with C's light row: in phases

Each phase can be merged on its own and leaves the page working. The full step list with files is in plans/race-day-redesign-plan.md.

0

Pure logic, tested first

  • New RaceDayGlance.cs: one record built from ProgramNextStep, NightPlan.For, FinalsCuts, MissionControl.Assess and the check-in store. It holds the next race, the coming-up list with clock times, the pace level, the light states and what's blocking the next race.
  • Pace thresholds: hidden when on time, amber at 10 min late, red at 30 (club setting). Tests cover no out-by time, a club night with no finals, and the case where no fix is enough.
  • The race name always leads with the class ("Stock · Q1 Heat 1"): a RoundName.WithClass helper, used on this page, Race Control's Next Up bar and the phones.
1

The pace alarm with fix buttons

  • A banner at the top of TodayControl, shown only when the pace level isn't on time. Each fix button runs the existing apply path with one undo entry and shows the minutes it saves.
  • The pace sentence is removed from the next-step detail line.
2

The Now card and Coming up list

  • Rebuild the next-step card: kicker, class-first name, four fact numbers, a blocker strip above Start, one primary button with Space. The demo hold (BuildDemoHold) stays.
  • A Coming-up list beside it in place of the Ready card: three rows plus "Full program ▸", each with its AboutOf time and one status pill.
3

The light row

  • Six fixed slots in place of the five stat tiles: Timer, Pace, Check-in, Marshals, Phones, Voice. Each draws a shape, a word and a colour. Clicking a slot opens its fix.
  • A club setting to turn a slot off for the night (for example, a practice night with no timer), so a red light always means something.
  • Start needs a 1-second hold while any slot is red (reusing HeldClickFilter).
4

The program drawer and footer

  • The program grid moves to a side drawer (like the existing timetable drawer). It opens scrolled to the next race, with the About column shown and cut races crossed out.
  • The footer goes from nine buttons to two secondary buttons plus quiet links, with right-click commands copied into a visible "More ▾". Start Selected moves into the drawer.
5

Check it at race-night conditions

  • Screenshot tests at 100/150/200% text size and at 1280 and 1920 wide. No sideways scroll, and Start doesn't move between states.
  • Contrast check on every pill (7:1 for anything you act on). A colour-blind simulation pass on the light row.
  • A timed run-through: 12 races on the demo timer, measuring seconds from "race recorded" to the next Start press, against today's page.
