# Release notes

HeatCaller is in beta. Version numbers follow **a.b.c**: *b* goes up when a whole feature lands, *c* for fixes and smaller additions.

## Unreleased

- **NASCAR-style broadcast: the lead change call now gives the count.** From the second change of a race on, the announcer adds *"That's the fourth lead change, among three drivers."* - the line only a booth watching the whole race could give. It is a tick box on the **Voice** page under *Leader change*, on by default, and it stays quiet when your own line already says `{leadchanges}`. New placeholders for writing your own: `{previous}` (who lost the lead), `{previouslaps}` ("6 laps" - how long they had it), `{leadchanges}` ("fourth"), `{leaders}` ("three") and `{backinfront}` ("back in front for the second time"). The count, the call and the stream's tower all come from one tracker, so they cannot disagree.

- **Driver introductions before the race, written by the AI commentary.** As a race is staged, the commentary rolls through the field in grid order - each driver's grid position, racing number, home club and a line of form - and before a main it opens with what qualifying decided: who took pole and by how much, from **tonight's** qualifying only, exactly as the grid sheet ranks it. The model only words it: every fact is worked out by HeatCaller and handed over exactly, and it is told to invent nothing - no numbers, towns or results a driver has not got, and no laps of a race that has not started. The form line is the most interesting true thing in their saved races: a milestone start, a first start, a winning run, won last time out, recent wins or podiums, or their average driver rating. It comes after the race intro and the Team Principal, last before the countdown, and **the automatic start waits for it**. **Turn it on or off** with *Driver introductions* on the **Commentary** page, or tick *Only for a main* to keep it for the feature races. A freeform race (no round picked) introduces whoever was on the board, without grid positions. **No wait for them:** they are written ahead as soon as the last result is filed, the same as the race intro and the Team Principal, and when the race goes **On Air** they are written alongside the opening and **rendered with it**, so they run straight on from the opening with no gap and the start does not wait for them at all. Demo races are introduced too, so you can hear it before a real night. When the introductions or the Team Principal are left out, the console now says why. It needs the AI commentary switched on; without it there are no introductions.

- **Fixed: a demo race now gets the whole show - the Team Principal before and after, and the Technical Lead after a main.** A demo stages the race itself rather than through the Stage button, so the round intro, the Team Principal before the start and now the driver introductions never ran for one; and a demo of a round already run (or any demo without *Demo races fill the program*) was left with no round at all, so the recap had no main to give the Technical Lead and no team for the Team Principal. **A demo of a round from Race Day is now that race again** - even one already run: the same drivers, the round's format (laps, time, start type), its track layout and its whole show. It is still filed as a demo, against no heat, so the round keeps its earlier result and nothing is scored. Its countdown waits for the show before the start the way a real race's does.

- **The co-host does the pit-lane interviews.** With a co-host switched on (Commentary page), the co-host now asks the questions - in their own voice - for the Team Principal and the Technical Lead after the race, and for the Team Principal before it. **After a race it is one interview:** one stinger, then the show host throws to the co-host (*"Let's head down to pit lane, where Jess is with the Team Principal and the Technical Lead from Sam's team. Jess?"*), the Team Principal answers first, and the Technical Lead follows straight on - no second stinger. **Before a race** the announcer throws to the co-host the same way (*"Before we go, let's head down to pit lane. Jess, you're with Sam's team."*), and the whole run before staging goes **opening, pit lane, driver introductions**. With **On Air** all three are written and rendered before the jingle and play back to back with no gap; with the Stage button they follow each other in the same order. The throws are fixed lines, rendered ahead with everything else, so they cost no wait. With no co-host, the host asks as before - still as one interview after the race, Team Principal first.

- **Battle calls say what the battle is for, and are on by default.** The *Close battle* call fills a new `{battle}` placeholder ("for the lead", "for third"), and a fight for the lead that the line does not mention starts *"Battle for the lead!"*. It used to ship switched off; new setups now have it on. **An existing setup keeps its own choice** - if you have never ticked *Close battle* on the Voice page, tick it now.

- **The stream's timing tower is now a proper pylon.** On the OBS graphics page (`/broadcast`): each row shows the car's racing number, the last column cycles every eight seconds between the **interval** to the car ahead, the **gap to the leader**, and **laps led** (once the lead has changed hands), and the header shows the lead change count. Pin one with `?gap=interval` or `?gap=leader`.

- **The battle box remembers the fight.** While it is the same two cars it shows how many laps they have been inside a second and whether the gap is **closing** or they are **pulling away**.

- **Banners for lead changes and milestones.** A lead change ("Sam takes the lead from Bill · lead change 4 among 3 drivers") and every achievement unlocked (first win, hundredth race, hat trick...) get a banner across the top of the stream for seven seconds - **even with the announcer muted**. Hide it with `?hide=moment`.

- **Sponsored segments: "the fastest lap, presented by...".** On the **Sponsors** page, each sponsor now has a *Presents* list: fastest lap, lead changes, battles, driver introductions, qualifying recap, the running order, milestones and the race result. Their name goes on the end of that call - *"Presented by Joe's Hobby Shop."* - and on the stream's graphic for it. **A moment that happens many times a race is named at most once every three minutes**, so the first fastest lap carries the name and the next four, a lap apart, do not; the graphic carries it every time, because a caption costs the room nothing. **The AI commentary carries them too:** the driver introductions open with *"Driver introductions, presented by..."*, the recap names whoever presents the race result, and a written battle watch, pace note, qualifying report or honours segment names the sponsor of its moment - under the same three-minute ration. A segment's mention is counted when it is written, since it goes out straight after. Two sponsors ticked for one moment is pointed out on the page - only the one higher in the list is named. **Every mention the announcer actually makes goes in the sponsor report** under that sponsor's reads.

- **Driver rating, 0 to 150.** HeatCaller's version of NASCAR's driver rating, built only from what the timing records: finish (55), a win (5), laps led (25), pace against the race's best lap (25), the fastest lap (10), distance (15) and places gained from the grid (15), less 5 a warning. From the back of the grid to a win, leading every lap with the fastest lap, is 150; flag to flag from pole is 142.5. **It does not use average running position**, which NASCAR leans on hardest - HeatCaller does not store the running order between crossings, and a rating that claimed it would be inventing it. Where you see it: a new **Rating** column you can add to any leaderboard (Race Control, Big Screen, web), each driver's rating on the stream's podium at the flag, **Avg rating**, **Best rating** and **Laps led** on the Driver Hub - and **in the AI recap**, which is now handed each finisher's rating, who led and for how many laps, and how many times the lead changed hands among how many drivers, and works in the one that tells the story of the race.

- **The commercial break now has a sound at its edges.** Your own show sting marks the way into the break, the way a station goes to the adverts. Coming back it stays out of the way: the recap already opens by saying you are back live at the track, and a grid call speaks for itself, so nothing is added in front of either.

  **The one case it does cover is the break that runs out.** If the break hits its ceiling with the recap still being written, or runs out of sponsors to show, the slides simply stop and the room is left with silence and no idea whether anything else is coming. That one gets the sting and a short line saying you are back at the track - deliberately *not* a promise about the recap, because a break that hit its ceiling is one where the recap may never arrive.

  **No new file to record.** It uses the show sting you have already set, at the level you set it. A club with no sting hears the break exactly as it does now.

- **The AI commentary can now say what a quick lap beat, and who held it.** The spoken *Track record* call has always named a new record when one falls. What the written commentary could not do is put a lap next to anything: the detector it reads takes its history as three questions it asks the app, and **two of the three were never hooked up** - the layout's all-time record and the fastest lap of the night were both permanently blank. So *"that takes two tenths off Dave's mark from the second heat"* was a sentence it was built to say and never could.

  Both are now wired. **The night's best is counted per layout**, which matters for a club that moves the track between classes: a lap set on a different configuration is not something the lap in front of you can be compared to, and calling it "fastest of the night" would be wrong about something the room watched. **Demo races never hold it either** - a club with "save demo races to history" ticked would otherwise have an invented lap holding the night's best for every real race after it - and nor do ghosts.

  **Known, and still open:** the third question - a driver's own best *before tonight* - is still blank, so "a personal best" means their best so far in that race rather than ever. Wiring it properly needs a cheap index rather than a question asked once per driver per lap; the roadmap has it.

- **A second dial for the announcer: how much ROOM it leaves between calls.** On the **Commentary** page, under *How chatty*, there is now a *Pacing* dial - five stops from **Room to breathe** to **Wall to wall**. It sets the shortest gap the announcer will leave between one timing call and the next, whoever they are about, and it is wide in practice and tight in the top main so the night still has a shape.

  **It exists because the chattiness dial could not have fixed this, and that was not obvious.** *How chatty* decides which calls are eligible and how many **one car** may make on **one lap**. Both of those are about a single driver, so three cars at a cap of two are six calls a lap and ten cars are twenty - and every one of them is inside the cap you set. Nothing in HeatCaller had an opinion about the field as a whole. Whatever arrived while the announcer was already mid-sentence was simply thrown away, first come first served, with no ranking at all: a change of lead could lose its place to somebody's lap time.

  **Race Control now shows you how many calls are not getting out.** At the end of the announcer row: *14 held* in grey, or *3 lost* lit up. **Held** is this dial leaving a gap on purpose - that is it working. **Lost** is the announcer running out of room for them, and the answer to that is a *wider* gap rather than a quieter one, which is the opposite of what you would reach for. The full count goes to the console at the end of every race.

  Those lines have always been written to the developer console and nothing else, which is why this went unreported for the whole life of the app - an eight-lap practice run with three cars in it was throwing away about twenty of them, and from the announcer row it just sounded busy.

  **Nothing that matters can be held.** A flag, a countdown, a green, a grid call, a driver's release, the finish, a badge, the last lap, the final minute, a track record and the running order all go straight past it - and so does any call that clearly outweighs the one before it, so a change of lead is never the thing that gets held.

  **This one does not default to "exactly as it was", unlike every other dial we ship.** "As it was" here means the twenty thrown-away lines above, and a room losing most of what the announcer tried to say is not a setting anybody chose. It starts on **Balanced**. The old behaviour is still on the dial as its last stop, **Wall to wall**, and the page says plainly what that costs.

- **The announcer says numbers like a commentator instead of like a computer.** A lap is now *"a five-oh-six"*, not *"5 point zero six seconds"*. A time that lands on the second is *"five flat"*. A gap is *"half a second"*, *"a tenth"*, *"a second and a half"* - and two cars crossing together is *"nothing in it"* rather than *"nought point nought seconds"*. A position said out loud is *"P four"*.

  It is the cheapest thing on the list of what makes an announcer sound like software, because it changes nothing about what HeatCaller knows - only how it reads it out. It was also sitting in a club's own console log, word for word, and nobody who has ever called a race has said that sentence.

  **A lap over a minute, or under a second, goes back to the plain reading.** *"Sixty fifty"* reads as six thousand and fifty, and a lap that long is given in minutes by anybody saying it out loud; under a second is a decoder fault rather than a lap. Sector times and session clocks keep the plain reading too - the oh-six form is a convention about lap times specifically.

  **The screens are unaffected.** They have always shown the numerals and still do.

- **The second voice can now be given a job rather than just a temperament.** A new co-host stance on the **Commentary** page: **Explains why**. The existing six give the co-host somebody to root for or a mood to be in - the underdog, the sceptic, the one who remembers everything. This one makes it the analyst: the one who says what the lap times *mean* and why it would be happening, while the first voice says what happened.

  **It will not invent a reason.** Where your results do not contain a cause, it says so instead of supplying one - which matters more here than for any other stance, because a made-up reason is HeatCaller telling a room why a driver was slow, wrongly, about somebody standing in it.

  It is offered first of the stances, because it is the one that suits any club rather than one with a particular taste.

- **Each driver can now be given their own night, at an address only their own badge opens.** On the **Club Website** page: *Give each driver their own night at a private address*. Every driver who raced gets a page of their own evening - their races, where they finished, how many they beat, their grid moves, their best laps and what the night's moments say they did. The address carries the twenty-character code already printed on their check-in badge, so there is nothing for them to sign up for, nothing for you to hand out that they do not already have, and no password to forget.

  **Off until you turn it on**, like every public route in HeatCaller. It is milder than most - it is text, and your results page already names everybody publicly - but it puts a named person's evening on the internet, and the shout-outs and the room's vote both ship off for less. An update does not get to decide that for your club.

  **Nobody without the code can open it, or find it.** The code is eighty bits worked out from the transponder under your club's own secret, which means it cannot be guessed and it says nothing about the car it came from. The relay answers everybody else identically whether the code is wrong, the driver did not race, or your club does not offer this at all - deliberately the same, because three different answers would let somebody working through guesses learn which codes are real. And one button on the check-in card reissues every badge in the club at once, if you ever need it to.

  **It needs online check-in set up**, because the badge is what the address is built from.

  **Every word in it is the announcer's own facts, unchanged.** Nothing rewrites, rounds or summarises - which matters because the one person reading it was standing there and can check all of it. It also means it works for *everybody* who raced rather than the two or three whose spoken piece you played: a written piece is made by being read out, and fifteen drivers would have opened a page with nothing on it.

  **It shows the full name** even if you have short names switched on for the club's public pages. A results table is a list of other people and shortening it is right there; a driver opening their own night and reading "B. Hargrove" has been shown a bug.

  **Unticking it takes every night back off the relay**, rather than just stopping new ones going up.

  **They find it from the check-in page**, under the Check in button, once they have scanned their badge: *See your racing from last time*. That is the only page in your club's site that already has their code, so there is nothing for you to send out - and the link only appears for a club that has this switched on, and only for somebody who actually scanned a badge rather than signing in with a PIN.

  **Their code stays out of every log it could land in.** The page reads it, asks for the night over a POST with the code in the body rather than in the address, and then rewrites the address into a form that browsers never send to any server at all - so a reload, a bookmark or a link they forward on keeps it out of access logs, out of referrers and out of a shared phone's history.

  **A Make a picture to share button**, and a Save this as a text file one under it. Both are built in the browser out of what is already on screen - nothing is stored and there is nothing extra to fetch.

  The picture is the 1200x630 shape a chat app or a club group draws as a banner rather than a thumbnail, in your club's own colours, with your club's name on it. It fills with as much of the night as fits and says "and 3 more" rather than quietly dropping the rest.

  **It is drawn on the driver's own phone, and that is the point rather than a shortcut.** HeatCaller already draws cards for a shared result, and pointing the same machinery at a driver's night was the obvious thing to do - and it is the one thing this feature must not do. A picture served from a web address has to be filed under something, and the only thing a night is filed under is the driver's own badge code. So the moment they posted it, that code would be in the post for anybody to read - and it opens their whole night and checks them in and out. The one thing the picture is *for* would have published the key to it. Drawn on the phone instead, a driver shares a picture and never an address.

  **Every kind of nothing reads the same.** A club that does not offer this, a driver with nothing filed, and a code that was mistyped all get one message that names both possibilities and says which it was for neither. Three different answers would let somebody working through guesses learn which codes are real, which is the one thing eighty bits are there to prevent.

- **A driver's own piece now opens with a few seconds of their own race.** The clips HeatCaller has been cutting for each driver out of each race's recording - the seconds they were actually called over - have been sitting in a folder on the laptop since recording shipped, and nothing played one. **Write And Play It** for a driver now starts with a line of handoff and then up to twenty seconds of their night as the room heard it, before the music and the written piece.

  Twenty seconds against a spoken piece of a minute and a half, and from the start of the clip, which is where the run-up to the moment is. A clip may be two and a half minutes long, and three of those around a minute of words is not a piece about somebody's night - it is the night played back.

  **It is in the saved recording too**, without anything being stitched together: it goes out through the same mixer the voice and the music do, with the recorder listening to the mixer.

  **Nothing about it can cost you the piece.** No recording tonight, the file gone, a disk that will not read, no clip for that driver - any of those and the piece plays exactly as it did before, with a line in the console saying which it was. Most drivers most nights have no clip, and that has never been a fault.

  **It follows the clip switch**, so a club that has unticked *cut a clip for each driver* does not get them used. A clip the encoder refused and left as a `.wav` is played too, which is every clip on a machine without Windows Media Foundation.

- **The announcer now remembers the last time two drivers met.** When two of them are racing each other again, it can say when they last did and what happened - *"the two of them were nose to tail in Heat 2 last week, and Bill came out in front"* - rather than treating every battle as the first. It reaches back a season and says how long ago in the way somebody in the room would: earlier today, yesterday, a few days ago, last week, three weeks ago, last season.

  **It only claims what the saved results prove.** It will not call something a fastest lap moment, because every race has one of those and it says nothing about the two drivers in question. A meeting that proves nothing names itself and no more.

- **Fixed: a private page on the relay could be fetched whole by anybody.** Channels on the relay can be marked as holding something private, and that mark was being honoured in one of the three places documents are read and ignored in the other two. Nothing had leaked, because everything private until now happened to be the kind of document the one working check covered - but the first per-person thing anybody added would have been served complete, to anybody, with no token at all. All three now refuse.

  The worse half was the listing. Asking a collection for its index returns every id in it, and for a driver's night an id *is* that driver's badge code - the thing that lets somebody be checked in. A listing would have turned the credential into a public list and handed over check-in along with it.

  Found by reading the relay rather than from anything going wrong, and fixed before the first feature that would have been bitten by it.

- **Fixed: a club that switched a public route off could have it filled straight back up.** Switching off listening along, or drivers' own nights, tells the relay to throw away what it is holding - and then the next thing the laptop sent was accepted anyway. For nights the relay was checking the switch when somebody *read* one and not when the laptop *sent* one, so the folder emptied and refilled a second later and the club's "no" meant nothing. Both ends now refuse, and a refused night is dropped rather than queued - a courtesy must never sit at the front of the queue your results are waiting in.

- **A shout-out that cannot be read out is now refused, instead of being accepted and thrown away.** If the night's list was already full, somebody sending a message from your club's page was told it had gone through - and then it was quietly discarded on the laptop, with nothing but a line in your own console to show for it. Somebody's dedication could vanish with a tick beside it. They are now told the announcer has plenty to get through already, in the same words a phone on the club Wi-Fi gets, and can try again once you have read a few out.

- **A sponsor's slide can no longer land on the crowd screen over another sponsor's read.** A commercial break starting while a sponsor's name was being said put the next sponsor's picture up on top of it - one sponsor's paid airtime carrying another's logo. The break now waits the few seconds for the read to finish.

- **A sponsor's picture comes off the screen when a read is cut short.** If the countdown started or the announcer was muted part way through a sponsor's read, their picture stayed up for the rest of its estimated length - up to forty seconds of a logo over a race going green.

- **The room's vote is now kept per race rather than per race name.** A club running two classes has two races both called "A-Main", and the relay could not tell them apart: the second one's vote opened with the first one's votes already in it. Votes are also no longer linkable between two races by the same voter, which was the other half of the same fault.

- **The room's vote is settled even with the AI announcer switched off** - it was being filed halfway through a step those clubs skip, so the room voted and never heard the verdict. **And a demo race can no longer settle a real vote** with an invented winner.

- **The Big Screen's band no longer reads "Sign-Off" during the night's recap**, or during a driver's own piece - which used to carry one driver's name in every sentence under a band reading "Sign-Off". It now says what is actually on.

- **Write And Play It waits for the flag.** Both the night's recap and a driver's piece could be started mid-race, taking the broadcast for four minutes of talking about results while cars were still deciding them.

- **Reopening HeatCaller mid-evening no longer makes the announcer start the night over.** If you closed the app between the heats and the mains - or force-closed it after a freeze - the announcer came back and said everything again: the same lap records, the same storylines, the same championship lines, all of it already heard. What it had already spent was remembered; what it had already *said* was not. Both are now.

  It is written down after every piece the announcer actually speaks rather than when the app closes, because the restart this matters for is usually not a planned one.

  It is also kept per race day, so last week's evening cannot leave this week's announcer with nothing to say.

- **Five cards on the Commentary page now line up with the rest of it.** The driver's cut, the night's recap, check-a-line, the chatter setting and the night before each had a caption column of their own width while the other fourteen cards shared one.

- **The night-before preview can now go on your club's page.** On the **Commentary** page, under *The night before*: *Also put it on the club's own web page*. The preview appears at the top of your club's home page - the date, what is on, how many are entered, and a reason to come - until the meeting starts.

  **The text file is still written either way**, and first. Nothing about this changes what the **Write The Preview** button already did; ticking the box adds the club's page to it. That is a separate tick on purpose: if you have been using the button all season to get something to paste into the club's group, an update should not quietly start putting the same words on a public address.

  **It takes itself down, which is the part that matters.** The preview comes off the page as soon as the first result of the day is filed. And because the likeliest ending to a race day is the laptop being switched off and taken home, the preview also expires on its own a few hours after the first race - so one left behind cannot sit on your front page describing last Saturday in the future tense. A preview written for Saturday survives Wednesday's practice evening; only the day it was written for takes it down.

  **A day with no start time set keeps its preview all day**, rather than losing it at six in the morning on the day of the meeting.

  It says the same things the file does and no more: who is entered rather than who is racing, nothing about who will win, nothing about any final's grid. Those do not exist yet.

- **Listening along from home - now the laptop sends it, and there is a switch.** On the **Commentary** page: *Let people listen along on the club's web page*. Tick it and a **Listen** button appears on your club's page; the announcer's lines go out as it speaks them, a few seconds behind the room. For a driver's family at home, somebody away for the weekend, or the member who had to work. There is nothing for them to set up and nothing for you to do once it is on.

  **Worth knowing before you turn it on:** this puts the voice you have chosen - including a voice cloned from somebody at the club - on an address anybody with the link can play. Switch it off and the button goes from the page and the relay throws away what it was holding. That is **sent**, not left to be worked out from the announcer going quiet: a club that has just said it does not want this should not find its announcer still playing to the internet.

  **It is not gated on the AI announcer being on.** With the AI off, what goes out is the lap calls, flag calls and results reads - which are the ones somebody at home most wants to hear.

  **Late is worse than missing, so it drops lines on purpose.** A lap call read ninety seconds after the lap is not late, it is wrong - it describes a pass that two more have happened since. If the connection cannot keep up, the oldest lines go and the newest are kept, and anything that has waited three quarters of a minute is thrown away rather than sent. Somebody listening on a bad connection hears gaps; they never hear your evening in the wrong order, and they never fall further behind as it goes on.

  **Nothing about it can cost you a result.** It has a queue of its own rather than sharing the one your results go in, which would have meant a busy evening of announcing quietly pushing a race's results out to make room for commentary.

  **A long piece will not go, and the console says so.** The end-of-night recap is over a minute of audio and more than one message will carry, so it is refused rather than cut off half way. Everything else - lap calls, flag calls, the between-race pieces - fits comfortably.

  The night's log now also says how many lines went out and how many did not make it in time, so a listener telling you it kept cutting out and your own screen are describing the same evening.

- **The night's log now says what was written in advance.** Intros written during the quiet between races are meant to be indistinguishable from ones written on the spot - which also meant that if a round's grid changed and the written-ahead intro was thrown away, nothing anywhere told you. The recap now reports how many were written, how many were used, and the reason for each one that was not.

- **Listening along from home - the club page side of it.** The relay and the club's own **Live** page can now carry the announcer, so a driver's family at home can hear the room. Nothing is switched on yet and the laptop does not send anything yet; this is the half that receives and plays it.

  **It is off until a club says yes**, like every other public route, and a club that has never been asked has not agreed. The reason is the one worth stating: a room's announcer saying something unfortunate about a ten-year-old's driving is a different matter in a hall and on the internet. The lines are **refused** by the relay with the switch off rather than politely not sent, because a switch on one laptop is not a guarantee - an older build, a restored backup, a second laptop set up with the same token. And **switching it off takes down what is already there**, because stopping sending would leave the last few minutes of an evening sitting on a public address.

  **It is not a stream**, and that is the design rather than a shortcut. Every line the announcer speaks is already a finished piece of audio, so the page simply plays them in order, one after the next - no encoder, and none of an encoder's ways to fail on a race night. Only the last few minutes are kept at all.

  **A Listen button, never autoplay.** A page that starts talking at whoever opens it is a page people close. And pressing it **joins the evening where it is** rather than replaying the last three minutes to catch up - the same as walking into the hall.

  Only the last few minutes are held, and they are held apart from everything else the club has on the relay: audio is thousands of times the size of a results sheet, and counted against a club's storage it would have pushed their saved results off to make room for last Tuesday's commentary.

- **Groundwork for a club announcing in its own announcer's voice - and two things that had to be fixed first.** Nothing is switched on for a club yet, and nothing in the app looks different. This is the relay side of it: a voice can now be marked as a **clone of a real person**, and marking it changes who is allowed to hear it.

  Two things that are exactly right for the stock voices turn out to be wrong the moment a voice belongs to somebody, and neither was obvious until a clone was on the table.

  **One: lines are shared between clubs, and a voice is not.** A line with nobody's name in it - *"Green flag!"* - is the same words at every club, so the relay renders it once and keeps it for all of them. That is why the HeatCaller voice is affordable. But the same words are not the same voice: in a cloned voice that recording is a named person saying it, and another club's flag calls would have gone out in your announcer's voice. For free, which is exactly how nobody would have spotted it. A cloned voice is now never shared - your own club's copy still works, so the only thing lost is a saving, and it is your voice so it is your saving to lose.

  **Two: voice samples are public, on purpose.** Anyone can listen to a sample of each HeatCaller voice without signing in to anything, because that is how you choose one. A cloned voice's sample would be a real person's voice on a web address anybody could fetch. Cloned voices are now left out of that list entirely, and asking for one directly gets the same answer as asking for a voice that does not exist - deliberately the same, because a different answer would tell anybody who asked which of a relay's voices are somebody's.

  The decision is made on the relay rather than in the app, and that is the part worth keeping: the app is never told which voices are clones of whom, so the rule cannot be broken by a club running an older version.

  **Still to come: the consent.** A cloned voice is a person's likeness, so the club holds the recording and the agreement, HeatCaller holds nothing but a voice id, and one button takes it back. Recording who agreed and when is the next piece, and nothing will speak in a club's own voice before it exists.

- **Something to post the night before.** New **Write The Preview** button under **Recording the night** on the **Commentary** tab: it writes a short preview of the race day you have set up - when it is, what is on, how many are entered in each class, the track's lap record, and what the championship has riding on it - and saves it as a text file in **Documents\\HeatCaller Previews** for you to paste into your club's page or group. It is the one thing HeatCaller makes on a day it is otherwise shut, and the only one whose job is getting people to the track rather than entertaining the people already there.

  **Everything in it is worded as a plan, and that is the whole difficulty.** Every other thing the announcer writes comes from saved results - things that happened, to people who were there. This comes from the entry list, which is what the club *means* to happen: three drivers will scratch, somebody will turn up who is not on it, and the programme will be reordered twice before the first heat. So it says who is **entered** rather than who is racing, that a race is **due out** rather than that it goes out, and how many cars are **on the entry list** rather than how many will be out. It never writes about any of it in the past tense.

  **Four heats of the same ten drivers is ten entries, not forty.** Worth saying because the other way round is the easy mistake, and it would have a club advertising a field four times the size of the one turning up. Pace cars don't count either - a count of cars is a claim about cars with people behind them.

  **It says less than it could, on purpose.** It will not tell you who is in any final, because those grids do not exist until the heats are in. It will not compare the entry against past fields - "the biggest Box Stock entry since March" counts entries against *starters*, which is near enough inside the hall and simply wrong the night before. It will not predict who will win, or what a result would do to a championship. And it gives no time for anything but the first race, because that is the only one a club commits to. A class with barely anybody entered is left out of the counts rather than advertised.

  **What it will say about a championship is the strong part**: *"two rounds of the Winter Series left, this one included, and it is between Bill Hargrove and Sam Teague."* That claim survives being a day early, because it is proved by playing a rival's best case against the leader's worst - and a rival who stays home scores nothing. It is read against the entry list, so nobody is named as a contender who is not entered.

  Posting it to your club's own web page automatically is not in this yet - a published preview has to come back **down** once the meeting starts, or it sits there in the future tense describing an evening that finished hours ago, and that is worth doing properly rather than quickly.

- **A race start no longer waits for the announcer to think.** The intro for a race is now written while the previous race's result is being filed, so by the time you press Stage the words are already there. Until now the staging chain held for up to twenty seconds while the model wrote - and on a slow or cold model it could hold longer than the start was willing to wait, which is how a countdown once managed to play twice. The console says which it was every time: *"Round intro was written ahead, so the start waits for no model."*

  **It can only ever save time, never cost a line.** A set of words written ahead that is still true saves the wait; one that is not is thrown away and the announcer writes a fresh intro exactly as it always did. There is no path here that makes a night worse than it was.

  **And "still true" is checked properly rather than assumed.** A held intro is thrown away if the programme has moved on to another round, if the grid changed - somebody scratched, somebody was added, or the order moved, because an intro that names the front row is wrong the moment the front row is not those two - if another segment has since said one of the facts it was built from, or if it has simply been waiting too long to be about tonight. Any one of those, and there is no "mostly still true". The console says which it was, so a club can see it working rather than wondering.

  **The timing of when it gets written is the whole correctness of it**, and it is worth saying why. The obvious moment is the green flag of the race before - the programme already knows what is next and who is in it. But a championship moves with every result: an intro written at the green of round four saying *"Bill leads by four points"* is wrong by the time Bill has won round four, and nothing could catch it, because a points total is not a fact that gets marked as said - it is just a different number. So it is written once the previous result is **filed**, never while a race is running. That still leaves the whole gap between the flag and the next call to the grid.

  It is one intro at a time - the next one, never a queue, because a programme reordered between races would mean several sets of words written to use one. A club that only has intros on for its mains and rivalries does not pay for the heats either. And nothing is written ahead for the first race of a night, which is the one start that still waits as it always did.

- **The announcer comes back to things it said earlier.** Until now every segment started from nothing: the show could call a fight for second place on lap four and then, two races later, have no idea it had ever mentioned it. Now it does. *"That fight between Carl and Dave we were on about earlier - Carl's taken it on the last lap."* It is the difference between an evening and a run of announcements, and it is the one thing on the broadcast that refers to the broadcast.

  **What is remembered is who was pointed at, never what was claimed.** This matters more than it sounds. "Watch Carl in the second half of this one" is not a prediction and cannot be wrong; "Carl will win" is, and HeatCaller must never say it. So the only thing written down is the SUBJECT - a driver, or the two sides of a fight - and the pay-off is whatever actually happened to them, worked out from the club's own saved result exactly as every other fact is. A subject the night had nothing to say about is dropped in silence rather than being paid off wrongly.

  **And where the two voices come back to something, nothing is claimed about what either of them said.** If your co-host has a point of view set, the callback says both voices were on it - which is true, because the segment went out as a two-hander - and nothing about which side anybody took. A brief that tells the second voice to be sceptical is an instruction to the model, not a record of what it did, and a club that heard the two of them agree about Carl and then heard the announcer say they had argued about him would be hearing it lie about the last five minutes of their own evening. The stance is still in force when the callback is written, so a sceptical co-host comes back to it sceptically without that having had to be recorded.

  **A segment is never paid off by its own race**, which is the hard part of this and the reason it took three attempts. A note called on lap five - "Carl has just gone quickest" - must not be answered two minutes later by "Carl set the fastest lap of that race", because they are the same lap and the announcer would be congratulating itself on reading out its own line. So a callback only ever uses a race that had not *started* when the earlier line went out. It also means a callback cannot chain into another one: coming back to a driver twice takes two races.

  It stays quiet rather than filling: one callback at most, never as a segment of its own, never in a race intro or a result read-out, and never in a segment that had nothing else to say - a reminder rides along inside something that is about the racing, and it is the first thing dropped when the sentence runs short. It holds twenty-five minutes' worth and then lets go, because "we were talking about that earlier" lands when earlier was two races ago and is a non-sequitur when earlier was before the break. Nothing is kept past the night, and the night's recap now reports how many subjects were pointed at and how many were come back to.

- **A piece about one driver's night, for that driver.** New **A driver's night** row under **Recording the night** on the **Commentary** tab: pick a driver, press **Write And Play It**, and the announcer gives about a hundred and fifty words on their evening alone - the races they ran, where they finished, what the night's own moments say they did. It plays out in the hall and lands in tonight's folder under their name, beside the races and the sign-off. It is the one thing a club can hand a single member to take home, and the thing a presentation night has never had.

  **It is written from the night's moments rather than from the results table**, which is the difference between "finished third" and something worth listening to. The app now reads each saved race back for the handful of things that were actually moments: a lead taken on the last lap, a finish inside a second, a charge of four places or more from where they started, a lap record falling on the club's own layout, and the fastest lap of the race *with which lap it was*. None of it is a new log - every one of them is arithmetic over the result the app already saved, so a piece written tonight and a piece written next February from the same file say the same thing.

  **Nothing in it invents a record.** A lap record counts as fallen only against a layout the club has actually recorded; a charge is counted from a real grid position, not from an assumed one; and where the saved result cannot honestly answer a question - who was ahead between two crossings, which the app does not store - the question is not asked. A piece about a driver who had an uneventful night says so rather than dressing it up.

  **One at a time, on demand, rather than a run through the entry list.** Twenty drivers at a hundred and fifty words each is half an hour of a hall listening to pieces that are not about them, and the only route from a written line to audio here is the announcer's own queue. So a piece is made by being played, and a club plays the ones it wants - at the presentation, or while the place is being packed up. The picker fills itself when you open it, newest race first, under whatever name your club gave the driver rather than whatever the timing system sent.

  It is **never shorter than a hundred and fifty words, whatever your per-line word limit is set to**, for the same reason the night's recap is never shorter than four hundred: a club that wanted terse race calls did not thereby ask for a two-sentence piece about somebody's evening. A club that has asked for longer lines than that gets the longer piece, because that club did ask.

- **You can ask why the announcer said the last thing it said.** New **Check a line** row under **Recording the night** on the **Commentary** tab, with a **Why It Said That** button: it shows which facts went to the model for the last line that went out, which were left out for want of room, which were held back because that theme had been used too recently, and what the money and airtime gates had to say. Until now the only account of any of this was a console line that scrolled past.

  **And the night keeps its own books.** The two commonest reasons a segment never went out were written down nowhere: a segment with nothing new to say and a segment whose facts had all been spent both returned quietly, so a club hearing a thin night could not tell whether that was three segments or thirty. The night's recap now reports the whole count - what was weighed up, what went out, and what each refusal was - which turns a quiet night into something you can read rather than something you guess at. The budget's reasons are now a fixed set rather than free text, so they can be counted instead of matched on their wording.

- **The announcer can say what the championship still has riding on it.** *"There are two rounds of the Winter Series left, this one included. Only Bill Hargrove and Sam Teague can still win it."* Or the one that settles an evening: *"Bill Hargrove has already won it on 148 points. Nobody left can reach that, even winning all two remaining rounds."* It goes in the race intro, which is the part of the broadcast with least to say and the part a room most wants framed.

  It needs one figure the app could not work out for itself, so there is a new **Rounds in the season** box on a championship's own rules, beside the best-of-N drop. Nothing else in HeatCaller knows how long a season is — a championship is a set of races that have *happened* plus a rule that takes more as they're recorded, and "best 5 of 7" is a drop cap, not a calendar. **Leave it at none and the announcer says nothing about any of this** rather than guessing, which is the right answer: a false "nobody can catch him" over a PA is the timing system telling a club something untrue about its own championship. It changes no points and no standings.

  **The claim is proved, not calculated, and that distinction is the whole feature.** The obvious way to answer "can Sam still catch Bill?" is the gap against the points still available — and it is wrong whenever a club runs a drop rule, because a driver's total then isn't their points added up. A real case: best 4 of 6, Bill has won four rounds and Sam has been second in four. That's 40 to 36 with two rounds and twenty points left, so every shortcut says it's still open. It isn't: Bill keeps his four wins whatever happens, and Sam winning both remaining rounds only swaps two 9s for two 10s, for 38. He is already mathematically out. So instead of arithmetic, each rival's **best case is played out against the leader's worst** — the rival winning every remaining round while the leader scores nothing — through the one function in the app that applies the drop rule. Drawing level counts as catching.

  It says nothing far sooner than it says something: a season too few rounds in, a title more than three drivers can still win (which is most of most seasons), a season already past its planned length, a scoring table where nothing scores. The console says which of those it was after every intro, so a quiet night reads as a decision rather than a fault.

So the one part of the rotation that costs the announcer airtime was the only part with nothing to look at.

  New **Picture for their read** row on each sponsor on the **Sponsors** page: choose a picture the club has already uploaded, or upload one for the read. An uploaded picture joins the slides above it, so you can see it and remove it there rather than it sitting in a folder you can't reach. **With no picture chosen the screen shows the sponsor's name**, large, for the length of the read — which is most of what a slide was doing for them anyway, and is what every sponsor gets until somebody uploads something. A picture that has since been deleted falls back to the name too.

  It holds the screen for about as long as the words take and then gives it back; nothing waits for a signal that might not arrive. A video keeps the screen if one is somehow up, because cutting an advert off part way is worse than the read going out over the standings, and the slide rotation stands down while a read is on air rather than moving to the next slide mid-sentence.

- **The room votes on who wins, and the announcer says what it reckoned.** A new **Votes** button on Race Control's announcer strip, beside Notes: open a vote when the race is called to the grid, and phones on the club Wi-Fi pick who they think wins at `/vote` on this PC's address. After the race the announcer holds the room's call up against the result — *"the room had Bill Hargrove at forty-eight per cent, and Bill has delivered"*, or the line that is worth the whole feature: *"Dave Bellamy has won it, and not one of the twenty people who voted had picked him."*

  **It is the only thing the announcer says that the room made itself**, and the only segment in the app that can be wrong. Everything else — the results, the laps, the records, the run of mains — is arithmetic over the timing, true by construction and nothing to do with the people standing on the bank.

  It stays quiet rather than overclaiming. Under five votes there is no verdict at all, because four people's guesses are not "the room". A room that split evenly picked nobody, so nothing is said. The figures go to the model exactly as counted, with instructions not to round a percentage or call a vote close, unanimous or overwhelming beyond what the numbers say — these are forty people who are standing right there and know what they voted. And nobody is made a fool of: the joke is on the room, never on a driver, whether the room backed them or not.

Two switches under **The room's vote** on the **Commentary** tab, both off until you turn them on. There is no approval list on either route and none is needed: a vote is a tap on one of the names your club put on the ballot, so unlike a shout-out nothing arriving can become a word the announcer says. The relay refuses any driver who is not on the ballot this PC published, and the worst a stranger with your link can do is be wrong about who wins. Switching the web-page route off tells the relay to stop taking them and to throw away anything it is holding.

  One vote per phone, changeable while voting is open and never doubled, and **no running tally is ever shown to a phone** — a room that can see what it is voting for is a bandwagon, not a prediction. The vote is keyed to the round rather than its name, so a club running Stock and Mod cannot have its Stock prediction read out over the Mod A-main. It closes itself when the result comes in, and again after twenty minutes whatever anybody pressed, because a prediction gathered after the flag is not a prediction. The Votes button carries the count as it climbs, so nobody forgets to close it. Switched off until you turn it on under **The room's vote** on the **Commentary** tab, and the announcer never says who anybody picked.

The player is now started quietly in the background as soon as the Big Screen sees that the club has a video slide, so by the time a break asks for one it is ready. A break that arrives before it is ready runs the club's picture slides for that slot and says so in the log.

- **An advert that starts late is not shown at all.** If starting a video takes long enough for the commercial break to end, the advert is dropped rather than played over the recap — an advert talking over the night's own programme is worth less to the sponsor than the same advert in the next break, which is where it goes. The log says how long a video took to start whenever it is more than a second.

- **A written sponsor read can no longer go out over a video advert.** Two adverts for two different sponsors over each other sells neither. The announcer's paid reads now also stand down while a sponsor's video is on the crowd's screen, and an ad-break read that is owed waits for the next real gap exactly as it does for a race.

- **Breaks now lead with a video even when a slide is already up.** The break used to ask for an advert only when the screen happened to be on the standings, which between races is about half the time — so a club that had loaded three videos saw one of them, once, all evening. A picture slide already showing has had its turn and comes round again; a video already playing still keeps the screen, because cutting an advert off part way is worse than anything else here.

- **The Big Screen says what each break leads with.** One line per break in the log: leading with a video, holding on one already playing, or no video to lead with and why — none loaded, or the files have gone. The report that started all of this came with a log that said nothing either way.

- **The colour now plays straight after the honours**, rather than after the pit-lane interview: the programme reads result, honours, colour, pit lane, goodnight, so the three things about the night sit together and the interview and sign-off close it.

- **The mechanic is introduced as the team's technical lead.** The announcer hands over with the role as well as the name — "Guiseppi, the team's technical lead" — and where no name is typed, by the role alone, which is a better handover than the bare "the winner's mechanic" it used to be. The setting on the Commentary page is still called the mechanic's name, because that is what you call the person when you are typing one in.

- **Why the form guide kept landing after the show.** It was not where it was being *placed* — it was that the ladder refused to hand it over while the programme was talking. Every segment has a minimum gap since the last spoken line, which is a good rule for filler between races and the wrong one inside a programme, where back-to-back is the whole idea: the programme's own previous line had always just closed that gap, so the segment was refused and then became due the instant the show went quiet. Asked from inside a programme, that one gate no longer applies. Everything else still does — switched off, already said tonight, already said for this class, nothing new to say, the ten-minutes airtime cap, and the month's allowance.

- **A sponsor's video only plays in a commercial break now.** The slide rotation used to play whatever came next at whatever moment its timer fired, video or still — and a video has its own soundtrack. On one race the club's advert played twice: once in the gap before the break had started, and again seven seconds into the recap, talking over the announcer reading the result. That second one can't be fixed by setting levels, because a video with sound deliberately ducks the club's music and then speaks over the programme. Videos are now held for the break, which is where a broadcast runs its adverts anyway. Out of a break the rotation steps over a video to the next **still** slide rather than showing nothing, so a club whose slides are mostly videos still gets a board; if every slide they have is a video, the standings stay up and the adverts get the screen the moment a break starts. Still slides are not affected at all — they have no sound, and a sponsor's logo up during a recap is exactly what they paid for.

- **The colour segments are part of the recap now, not three more broadcasts after it.** The form guide, the grid history and whatever else the announcer had due used to arrive *after* the programme had signed off — each one opening a show of its own, with its own opener and its own music bed. A club heard the recap, the honours, "and that's a wrap", and then two more mini-broadcasts. They are now items inside the recap's own show, after the honours and before the sign-off, with the same mark between them that a show puts between its own parts: result, honours, pit lane, colour, goodnight. One programme. Two of them at most, because past two a programme about a ninety-second race becomes the reason the next heat is late. They are written while the recap is being spoken, so they cost no extra silence, and a race called to the grid drops them like everything else. **Segments during a race and before one are untouched** — a battle call over a running race and a round intro before one happen exactly where they always did.

- **The host signed off, and then threw to the honours.** Confusing, and it was half a fix rather than a new fault: the recap's sign-off was correctly held back to come last, but the instruction that tells the model *whether to sign off at all* was still only asking about the mechanic's interview. On a race with badges and no mechanic the model was told to "sign off the race", did so, and the host then handed over to the honours reader straight afterwards — a programme ending and then carrying on. Anything following the recap now stops it signing off.

- **The show comes with music now.** Seven music beds and seven openers ship with HeatCaller, and a fresh install starts on one of each — so the opener and the bed are there the first time you tick the show on, instead of being a job you have to do first. Pick from the new drop-downs on each row under **The show's music** on **Voice → Commentary**: beds are three minutes each with tempo in the name (Opening Drive 132 BPM, Halftime Heat 92 BPM, Hard Count 150 BPM and so on), openers are the short hits that land in front of the voice. **Your own files still win.** Nothing about **Choose...** has changed: set a file of your own for a part and that is what plays, the drop-down for it greys out to say so, and **Remove** puts you back on the shipped piece rather than on silence. A club that had already dropped their own `opener.*` or `bed.*` into the show music folder is left exactly as they were. The sting — the mark between two parts of a show — deliberately starts at none, because nothing that ships is short enough to be a mark and a swell of the bed does that job better; you can still pick one of the openers for it if you want.

  All fourteen pieces were levelled to the same loudness on the way in. That matters more than it sounds: nothing in HeatCaller normalises audio, and the opener/bed/duck percentages were tuned against material at a particular level — so a hotter bed would have sat on top of the announcer rather than under him, and switching beds would have changed your whole mix. They are all the same now, so the levels you set by ear hold whichever bed you choose.

- **The pause when the second voice came in.** A two-hander — the honours reader, the co-host, the mechanic — had about a second and a half of dead air at every change of voice, which is enough to stop it sounding like two people and start it sounding like two announcements. Almost none of it was deliberate. Every line is padded with silence at both ends to stop its start or finish being clipped: 0.9s behind and 0.15s in front. Between two turns of one exchange both were being charged in full, and the deliberate beat at the change of voice sat on top of them — so the beat was a third of a gap nobody had ever measured. The pads are now trimmed to nothing between two lines that run straight into each other (the long one exists for exactly this and had never been used anywhere), and with them gone the beat is audible as itself for the first time, so it has come down from 450ms to 180ms — about where a person comes in on somebody else. **The gap at a change of voice goes from roughly 1.55s to about 0.25s.** The mechanic's interview gained the most: it has no beat of its own, so the whole gap between a question and its answer was padding, and it is now about a quarter of a second.

- **The announcer now has a name, and he introduces himself.** There is a new **Announcer's name:** box under **Broadcast** on **Voice → Commentary**. Type one and he welcomes the room and says who he is at the cold open and at the top of the recap - once, the way a presenter does, and nowhere else. Leave it blank and he is never named. This also fixes something odder: every other voice in the broadcast could already be named - the mechanic, the honours reader, the co-host - and the one voice carrying the whole programme could not. So when a club ran the two-voice segments, the model was writing dialogue between a named person and an anonymous one, and it did the obvious thing: it gave him a name of its own and had the co-host use it on air. A club heard "And Asher's still within striking distance, Laura" twice in one race. There was no Laura. Where you leave the box blank, the model is now told plainly that the voice has no name and must not invent one.

- **The sign-off is the last thing you hear.** It was only ever held back for the mechanic's interview, and the honours were invisible to it - so on every heat the recap signed the programme off itself ("and that's us, next up the A-Main, goodnight") and the badges were then read out afterwards. From the floor it sounded exactly like that: the honours arriving after the show had ended. Anything that follows the recap now pushes the sign-off out to its own line at the end. Nothing to set.

- **Two shows could play over each other.** Race Control runs the whole programme after a flag - teaser, recap, honours, mechanic, sign-off - and the Announcer screen's director independently looks for a segment to fill the gaps between races twice a second. Nothing held the two apart. On a club night the recap opened its show, and nine seconds later the director opened the night's **sign-off** on top of it: two openers, two music beds, mixed together over one voice, and the programme signing off before it had read the honours out. The post-race programme now holds the broadcast for its whole length and the filler stands down while it does. Nothing is lost by standing down - a segment that goes unsaid is unspent - and the gap after a recap is long. Paid sponsor reads respect it too, including in the quiet stretches between the recap's own lines, which were a gap a read could previously drop into.

- **The announcer promised one honour too many.** The throw to the honours reader says how many are coming - "three to hand out" - and that count was the number of *awards* rather than the number of *badges*. The two differ whenever one badge goes to more than one driver, which the date-based badges do constantly: Weekend Warrior and Back to Back fire for everybody who raced both days. So a race where two drivers both took Weekend Warrior had the announcer promise three and the room then hear two badge names. It now counts distinct badges. Where the read's cap bites, the console reports both figures, so a read that comes out shorter than the log has an explanation.

- **Play again, and Skip, on Race Control.** The announcer strip under the Next Up bar had one button, **Hold**, which stops the *next* thing. Everything already said was final and everything in progress ran to its end whatever happened in the room. There are now two more. **Play again** puts the last segment out again, with its show round it - for a line the room talked over, a PA that cut out, or somebody who missed the honours. It costs nothing: no model request, nothing spent off your allowance, and it works with the AI switched off at the wall, which is exactly when you are most likely to want it. A two-voice segment goes out as two voices rather than as one voice reading a transcript. **Skip** stops the show that is on air - the bed rides down and the rest of the running order is dropped - and deliberately lets the sentence being spoken finish: cutting it off would mean clearing the voice queue, and that is where the checkered flag, the countdown and a driver's release call all sit.

- **The strip says which segment just played, not what it said.** It used to print the first ninety characters of the last line, in quotes. That was the wrong thing on the busiest screen in the app: you heard the line, or you were busy, and a wall of text in a one-row strip is not how anybody catches up - and it shoved the buttons sideways as it grew and shrank. It now reads "Last: Race Recap", "Last: Club Honours", "Last: Cold Open", which is what tells you where the show is up to. A two-voice segment says so.

- **Why your sponsor's video did not play after the race.** Three separate things, and the first was the real one. The commercial break gave up silently when **Play the sponsors as a commercial break while the recap is being written** was switched off - which is the likeliest reason of all, since it is off until you turn it on - so a club asking the question had a log with nothing about a commercial break in it anywhere. It now says so. Second, a break is usually about one turn of the slide rotation long, so with the slides taken in file order a club's single video advert came up on a break roughly never: the stills either side of it had the eight seconds. A break now **starts** on a video where you have one and fills the rest of itself with the stills, searching forward from wherever the rotation is so two videos take turns across the evening. Third, a video that did get its turn could be cut off part way, because the break ended as soon as the recap was ready: a video actually playing now holds the break open until it finishes, under the same 90-second ceiling, so you do not sell a thirty-second spot and show eight seconds of it.

- **Video adverts say what happened to them.** The video path had no logging at all. It worked out a careful reason for every way it could fail - the video player's files missing from beside HeatCaller.exe, the player refusing to start, a file it could not read - and then threw every one of them away, so a club whose advert did not appear could not tell "this PC cannot play video" from "that file is broken" from "the rotation never reached it". The Big Screen now reports which video is playing and whether with its sound, that video cannot play on this PC at all with the reason (said once, not every time the slide comes round), and which file the player turned away, by name.

- **"He was a second off this in practice."** The announcer can now compare a driver's race pace tonight with their **practice** pace tonight. This fills a real gap rather than repeating the figure that was already there: the existing one compares a race with a race, so it needs a driver to have been out twice and says nothing before the third race of a class, where this one needs one practice run and one race and so exists at the second. It is scoped as "in practice tonight" and never to a class, deliberately - a practice session has no class, so HeatCaller cannot know whether the driver practised the car they are now racing, and the line says only what is true. **Practice is still not a race:** it is read separately, and nothing else has started counting it - not the field strength, not the form guide, not the championship, and not a start in anybody's career. A club that does not run its practice through HeatCaller hears nothing about practice, exactly as before.

- **A recap that overran said nothing about it.** Each part of the post-race programme waits up to 90 seconds for the one before it to be heard, then carries on regardless - which is right, because the honours and the sign-off must not be lost to a long recap. But it did it silently, so a night where everything after the recap arrived bunched up behind it looked, in the log, exactly like a night where each part was heard out properly. It now says so.

- **Five things the announcer offered and could never say.** Under **Segments** on **Voice → Commentary** there are tick boxes for things the announcer covers beyond the intros and recaps. Five of them did nothing at all. **Form guide** ("How the drivers in a main have been going lately."), **Halfway standings** ("Where things stand partway through the heats."), **This day at the club** ("What happened here on this date in other years."), **Cold open** ("Sets the evening out before the first race.") and **Sign-off numbers** ("The night's totals, fastest lap and closest finish.") each had a box you could tick and nothing behind it, so ticking one bought silence and nothing ever said why. All five work now. The form guide reads a driver's last few finishes in that class out in order, most recent first, so it is your own saved results rather than a verdict on anybody. The halfway standings go out partway through the heats, once or twice a night per class. "This day at the club" looks for what happened at your track on this date in earlier years. And the two ends of the evening finally exist: the cold open counts the night up before a wheel has turned - how many classes, how many drivers, how many races still to run, and where, if you typed a venue on the race day - and the sign-off reads back the numbers nobody in the room was counting: races run, laps turned, the fastest lap of the night and who did it, the closest finish, if there actually was a close one, and how many drivers raced at your track for the first time. Practice slots are left out of the cold open's count of races, deliberately: a night of ten heats and twenty practice sessions is ten races, not thirty.

- **The announcer has four new kinds of thing to talk about, and none of it is made up.** Race intros used to have the grid and very little else, so two races with the same ten drivers sounded the same. They now also get **the track's lap record** - the outright best on the layout you are running, who holds it and when it was set; **how strong the field is** - the biggest field this class has had at your club and how many races back that was, and how many of the drivers in this race have won a main in that class at your club this year; **how the evening is going** - what has happened to a driver's pace since their first race of the night, measured the same way as the track evolution figure on the **Next Up** bar so the two can never disagree; and **the car**, from the **Garage** - which only the mechanic's interview ever mentioned before, and which the recap now has as well, along with where tonight's result sits in the winner's own record ("that is his first win here", "he has never finished this high"). Every one of them is counted out of your club's own saved results, because a record a driver knows is wrong costs you the rest of what the announcer says. Where the app cannot count something, it says nothing instead of guessing.

- **The night is told as one story now.** The announcer picks one storyline at the cold open - a driver who has won the last race of the last few race days, say - refers back to it between races, and resolves it at the sign-off, so an evening has an arc instead of being nine unconnected races with announcements over them. Two things make it safe to listen to. The shape is invented but the claim inside it never is: "can anybody stop that run tonight?" is a question, and the run it is asking about is counted out of your filed results or there is no storyline at all. And nothing about it is remembered, which is the point - it is read fresh every time, so the moment somebody beats the driver the story was about, the story turns instead of carrying on asking a question the room has already answered. A night with no shape to it, or a club with too few race days or too few drivers on file, gets no storyline rather than a manufactured one.

- **The announcer can say what a race would decide - with the condition said out loud.** A new line in a race intro: *"if Sam wins this one and Bill is second, Sam takes the lead of the Winter Series from Alex, by three points."* It is only ever offered as a named finishing order, never as "a win here and he leads the championship", and the difference matters: what a result does to a table depends on where everyone else finishes, and a flat version of that sentence is true for one finishing order out of many. Before it says anything, HeatCaller scores the hypothetical through the same championship machinery your standings come from - your points scheme, your "best N of" drop rule, your excluded drivers - and then prices every other driver in the race at the best they could possibly do, including retiring, and checks the claim still holds. If it does not hold for every arrangement of the rest of the field, nothing is said. The margin in points is only given when it is the same margin whatever the rest of the field does; otherwise the line says the lead changes hands and gives no number. Level on points is not leading, so a hypothetical that only draws level is refused too. It needs a championship with a few rounds already counted and at least two of its drivers in the race, so it stays quiet far more often than it speaks - and when it does speak, the condition it was proved for is written in the console beside it, so you can account for anything the announcer said after the night.

- **The sponsors play as a commercial break while the recap is being written.** The AI recap takes the model a while to write - well over half a minute on a slower PC - and all that used to cover it was the teaser ("Give us a second and we'll tell you how that one unfolded."). Now, with **Play the sponsors as a commercial break while the recap is being written** ticked on **Voice → Commentary**, the finish call and the teaser are followed by a proper break: the Big Screen hands its rotation over to your sponsor slides and videos, the **ON AIR** band reads **COMMERCIAL BREAK**, and the recap comes back from it on your sting - which is what a sting is for. The break is exactly as long as the model took. It never runs under eight seconds, so it cannot look like a glitch, and never over ninety, so a model that has hung cannot keep the standings off the crowd's screen all evening; it ends early if it has been round every sponsor you have, and it is abandoned at once if the next race is called to the grid. Nothing is added for a club without it: with no sponsor slides, with them switched off, or with no Big Screen open, the night runs exactly as it did before. Your music keeps playing under the slides - a break of still pictures with no sound is dead air - and a sponsor video with sound quiets it as it always did.

- **Three smaller things in the announcer.** The show no longer opens before there is anything to open for: the music used to start before the recap arrived, so on a slow night the crowd heard the opener land, ring out, and then eighteen seconds of music bed with nobody talking over it. The opener now waits until the recap is written, and a race the model had nothing to say about opens no show at all. **Grid history** is retuned, now that race intros carry the same history themselves: it comes round less often, and a debut or a head-to-head is announced once an evening rather than once a race - six heats with the same ten drivers used to mean the same debut announced six times. And the console no longer fills up with one line: a segment the announcer has run out of budget for is now reported once, not on every half-second pass, which on a busy night was hundreds of copies of the same sentence burying everything else in the log.

- **The robotic Windows voice is gone, and Kokoro is the free voice for everyone.** **Voice → Voice Settings** no longer offers the Windows voice, and nothing falls back to it. Kokoro - natural-sounding, free, running on your own PC, costing nothing however much the announcer talks and needing no account and no internet - is what a new club gets and what speaks whenever the voice you chose can't: no ElevenLabs key, no Wi-Fi, a spent quota or a used-up HeatCaller allowance all land on Kokoro instead of going quiet. If your settings said **Windows voice** you come up on Kokoro with nothing to do, and your old pick of a Windows voice for the co-host or the mechanic is simply forgotten. **If you pay ElevenLabs by the character you stay on ElevenLabs**, deliberately: a club that had a key *and* had chosen the free voice is not moved onto the one that charges them. The one thing worth knowing is that Kokoro is now the bottom of the ladder, so if its voice file is missing from your PC the announcer says plainly - in the console, on the **Mixer** as a red NO VOICE, and on Voice Settings - that it cannot speak, and shows you the one button that fixes it.

- **Hear a voice before you choose it.** Voice Settings has a **Hear It** button beside the HeatCaller voice list: it plays a short sample of the highlighted voice, free, and it works *before you have signed up for anything at all*, so you can listen to every voice on offer and then decide. The first person anywhere to listen to a given voice has it made once; everyone after hears the saved copy for nothing. Beside it is a box for your own words and a **Preview** button, which has the announcer say your actual class names and driver names in that voice - because a stock sample cannot tell you whether it gets "Stock Buggy A-Main" right. Previews come out of a small daily allowance of their own, **kept completely separate from your race-night voice**: an afternoon of auditioning can never leave you short on Saturday, and when the day's previews are gone the app says exactly that rather than quietly spending the other budget. This closes the oldest item on the known-issues list. **The samples and the Preview button are served by the relay, so the relay needs redeploying before either works:** get the new relay code and run `fly deploy --ha=false`.

- **Check how a driver's name is read, before the night.** A driver's **Announcer name:** box has always been read instead of their real name; it now says plainly that you can spell it the way it *sounds* - put "Zhock" in and the announcer says Jacques properly, while he stays Jacques on the heat sheet, the results and the website. New beside the box: **Hear It**, which speaks the name exactly as the announcer will, in the voice your club is set up with, from what is in the box right now - no saving, no race. And **Check Names...** on **Driver Hub** opens a window that lists everyone the roster is showing, with a tick box to narrow it to whoever is checked in tonight, and reads them out one press at a time with the words it is saying on screen. Three wrong names get fixed in five minutes before the doors open instead of over the PA. **Hear It** is on the Add Driver window too, which is where a transponder nobody recognised gets named at seven o'clock.

- **A show is one piece now, with punctuation between its parts.** A show used to be a run of separate announcements that happened to have music under them. It is now a single block: it opens once, says its two or three things with a deliberate mark between them, and plays out. The marks are the three a radio station uses - your **sting**, a new third file beside **Opener:** and **Bed:** on **Voice → Commentary**, put in between two parts that have nothing to do with each other; the bed **swelling** for about a second where one thought leads into the next; and a plain beat of silence, about two thirds of a second, where the words themselves carry the change. Nothing is put in front of the first part, and no mark goes between every pair, which is exactly what would make it sound mechanical. A block is held to a minute and a half, and the joining lines are the first thing dropped if it would run longer - never a race call. And if a race moves on while a show is talking - staged, counting down, a green or any flag - the block gives up at once and the next thing the crowd hears is the race.

- **Two voices hand over to each other.** With a co-host set up, every change of voice inside a show now gets a beat of air - just under half a second - and the music bed nudges up underneath it, which is the sound of a studio handing over rather than two people talking over a loop. It happens inside a show only, so an ordinary race call is not slowed down by a fraction of a second.

- **HeatCaller writes the lines that join a show together.** The sentences that carry the crowd from one part of a show to the next - a look back at the race just run, what is still to come, the first race of the night, the last one - are written by HeatCaller itself, not by the AI model. So they cost nothing, need no setting up, and keep working when the model is slow, busy, unreachable or not installed at all. Each kind has several phrasings, drawn out of a shuffled bag so the same sentence is never said twice in one night, and the car count multiplies them again. Nothing in them is invented: the class names and the numbers come from the program you built and the results you filed, which is why they need a race day open with its program on **Race Day → Next Steps** and the commentary switch turned on. A quiet moment between two races can now be one of these lines on its own, carried on your own theme.

- **The show no longer sounds the same every time.** The first show of the night plays your opener from the top, as it always did - but the ones after it **come in on the tail of the same music**, so it is unmistakably your theme and it is over in about a second instead of four. That is how a radio station keeps a theme from wearing out over three hours, and it answers the complaint the guides themselves admitted to ("you'll hear the opener before every heat, which gets old over a long night"). A show after a quiet spell re-establishes the theme without making a meal of it, and the sign-off gets a full open and the bed held up on its own after the last word. When the announcer stops talking the music now comes **up** for a beat before it finishes, which is the sound of a programme ending rather than the music running out, and a beat of air sits in front of the opener so it no longer lands in the same instant your playlist stops. One new tick box on **Voice → Commentary** if you would rather hear all of your opener every single time, and the page tells you, in your own file's seconds, exactly where a later show will come in.

- **The Big Screen says ON AIR while the show is on.** A band comes up across the foot of the Big Screen, under the ON DECK bar, the moment a show starts: a steady broadcast-red **ON AIR** block, and beside it on a plate what is being read - **RACE RECAP**, **THE GRID**, **STANDINGS SO FAR**, **SIGN-OFF** and so on. It wipes on from the left and then sits still. Nothing blinks and nothing crawls, because something moving while the crowd is trying to read the leaderboard is worse than no band at all. It delays nothing and covers nothing that matters - the leaderboard is the only thing that gives up any room, and everything else stays where it is, the grid roll call deliberately included, so a show over a staged race keeps both on screen. It comes in every look, with or without your logo, on every Big Screen you have open. Press <kbd>O</kbd> to turn it off, and unlike the track map (<kbd>M</kbd>) that choice is remembered between sessions. It needs your show music set up: with no opener and no bed there is no show to be on air with.

- **A sponsor can have a read, not just a mention.** The **Sponsors** page takes a few sentences of proper copy for each sponsor, beside their slides and their videos, with a live hint telling you about how long it takes to read. You choose where it is heard: at the end of an ad break once their video has finished, in the gap between races, both, or neither. The announcer reads your words exactly as typed, so it costs nothing to run and **works with the AI commentary switched off entirely**. Copy longer than the gap finishes in the next one rather than being cut off mid-sentence; a race call always comes first and a recap or a "coming up next" always outranks an advert; and if you sell four slots all four really get read - HeatCaller keeps count and shares them out by whatever share each sponsor bought.

- **Every race is saved as an mp3, with a clip for each driver.** HeatCaller records the race as it goes out - the announcer, the effects, the stingers and the sponsor reads, but not your music, which plays on its own and never passes through the part being recorded. When the race and the after-race chat are done it writes the whole thing to **Documents\HeatCaller Race Audio**, a folder per night, named with the date, the time and the race so a file from three weeks ago is obvious without opening it. It also cuts each driver a short mp3 of just the moments they were named - their fastest lap, where they passed somebody, a penalty, and for the top three the finish and the interview - so you can hand a driver their bit of the night. Those moments are worked out from the lap times already saved with the result, so there is nothing new to keep and nothing new to go wrong. It cannot hurt a race night: capped at forty minutes, it gives up by itself if the disk runs low, it never goes near the laps or the results, and if the PC is closed or crashes mid-race the part already recorded still plays and becomes an mp3 next time. **Race audio is deliberately left out of your backups** - it is far too big, exactly like the photo-finish pictures - so copy that folder yourself if you want it kept. There is no tick box for it yet: it is on, and the race log prints the exact folder it is writing to.

- **Close battles and record laps can be called again.** The two things that watch a race as it runs - the one that spots a fight holding for half a minute, and the one that holds a record lap until there is room to say it - were being wiped twice a second by the housekeeping that starts a new night. Both needed to accumulate for far longer than that, so **neither could ever fire in a real race**, while passing their own tests. They are reset per race now, as they always should have been.

- **The twenty build warnings are gone.** Eighteen of them were one complaint, repeated: the main window's constructor is about 1,490 lines long and the compiler gives up following it, then reports every store it could not follow as possibly unset. They are all set, unconditionally, and the warning is now turned off for that one constructor with the reasoning written down beside it - rather than by marking eighteen fields as nullable, which would have silenced every genuine warning about them for ever and told the next reader they can be null, which they cannot.

- **The sidebar narrows to just its icons.** Click the chevron beside the **Search pages...** box, or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>B</kbd>, and the sidebar folds down to a rail of icons - about a quarter of its width, with the rest going to the page beside it. On a 1366-wide laptop at the track that is a column of the leaderboard back. Nothing is hidden: every page is still there, in the same order and the same groups, the page you were on stays on screen, and dragging a page still reorders it. What goes is the words - the page names, the group headings, the search box and the **HEATCALLER** header, which becomes **HC**. Hover over an icon for its page's name, and for its count where it has one: the **Rentals** count becomes a dot in the icon's corner with the number in the tooltip. The three buttons at the bottom stack into a column. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>F</kbd> opens the sidebar first and then puts the cursor in the search box, since the box is not there to type into. How you leave it is remembered, so a club that prefers the rail gets it at every start. It is the everyday version of Race Mode (<kbd>F11</kbd>), which hides the sidebar outright and shows only Race Control. See [Just the icons](getting-started/tour.md#just-the-icons).

- **Fixes from a fifth bug hunt.**
  - **The show, and the club's music coming back:** the club's music now always returns after a show. A round intro's show takes up to three seconds to open, and a race leaving staging inside that window - a countdown started by hand, a reset, a stop, a red flag - used to cut a show that wasn't there yet: the real one landed a moment later and was never ended, so the music stayed muted and the bed looped under the countdown, the horn and every heat for the rest of the evening. A show whose start is called off during the opener - the next race called to the grid over a recap's teaser - now stops its bed as well as giving the music back; it used to leave the bed looping under the playlist all night. A show beginning on one thread while another ends can no longer be reported in the wrong order and leave the music muted, and a fault in anything listening for a show no longer stops the show ending. Two shows open at once (the announcer's director and Race Control share one) no longer overwrite each other's view of whether the announcer is talking. An absolute path left in the show's opener or bed is ignored unless it is a real local path, so a planted network path can't have Windows log on to another machine when the **Commentary** page opens.
  - **Race night:** a sponsor video the grid roll call takes the screen from now stops. It used to keep playing: its sound over the start of the race, and the music muted for the whole heat. **Delete** on the program's right-click menu now names the rounds you've picked that have already been run and says their results stop counting; it took them while reassuring you nothing would be lost. **Close Day** asks first, says how many rounds haven't been run, what stops working and how to put it back, and refuses while a round is on track - as **Delete Day**, the button beside it, always did. **Open Big Screen** brings the window already up to the front instead of opening a second one over it. Undoing a deleted timetable line puts that line back, with its time, its place and its tick, and leaves the rest of the sheet alone; it used to take back everything done to the sheet since.
  - **Results and championships:** undoing a **Reset** no longer books the next crossing after the green as a lap as long as the stoppage. The race clock moved on but the decoder's clock and every driver's readings on it stayed where they were, so a lap the length of the stoppage and a jumped elapsed time went into the saved result and the journal. Championship standings now come out in the same order every time: two drivers exactly level on points, wins and best finish are settled on a count-back of podium finishes, where they used to fall in whatever order the night's races happened to be read in - which also made the end-of-race swing report show a place change for somebody whose points hadn't moved. **A championship with two drivers exactly level may re-order once with this build.** A second correction to a saved result no longer records the renumbered place as the one the race recorded, which could settle a tie the wrong way round.
  - **Timing, and the freeze:** HeatCaller no longer stops responding while it looks for a decoder. **Connect when HeatCaller starts** retries every few seconds, and the open itself - listing USB devices for an EasyLap, or closing and reopening a COM port for an I-Lap - ran on the window's own thread with no time limit. A decoder wedged on the bus with its device still listed, a Bluetooth serial port that takes half a minute to pair, or an adapter pulled out of its socket each stop inside the Windows driver with no error and almost no work being done, and the window stops with them. This is the leading explanation for the ten seconds rehearsal 2 lost in September, and for why HeatCaller watches itself for freezes at all. The opens now happen off that thread, one at a time. The I-Lap's raw capture also wrote each line from the decoder straight to disk while the window waited on the same lock to disconnect, and each lap was handled while holding it.
  - **Announcer and commentary:** the **Qualifying Report** names tonight's TQ. It counted every heat the class had ever run. The co-host now works on a local Ollama model: the two-hander's **A:** tag and its line breaks were stripped before the two parts could be told apart, so the exchange came out silently as one voice and paid for a second write on top. On a PC that writes decimals with a comma, lap times, gaps and margins went into the prompt as "12,345s" and came back out of the PA as "twelve thousand three hundred forty-five seconds". The director now cools off the battle it just called instead of picking another one, keeps its facts when a hold comes on part-way through a write instead of spending them, and works out every segment's facts before the first request goes out. **Green (Start)** pressed with nothing staged no longer reads the last race's recap over the green race: a flag now drops the recap along with the rest of the queue, where it alone used to be spared.
  - **Music and crowd sound:** the music and the crowd no longer stall the window while they open or close a sound device. Both held the lock the window reads whether the music is playing and whether the crowd is running through, several times a second, across opening the file, opening the output and closing it again - and opening an output device is the slow thing the app's shared sound output exists to avoid. A playlist whose files won't play now tries each one once and stops instead of going round again.
  - **Your data:** a settings file holding just `null` - four bytes, which a half-finished write or a sync tool can leave behind - is now treated as unreadable, so HeatCaller keeps a copy aside, holds its writes and says so. Race history, the race program, the driver roster, track layouts, handicaps and the music library used to read it as a fresh install without a word, and the first change of the night wrote an empty file over the club's data.
  - **Phones and Local Screens:** the Live Results page no longer gives away transponder numbers. Any phone on the club's Wi-Fi can fetch the spectator board with no PIN - that is what it is for - but every row on it, every car on the track map and the whole **Follow a driver** list, including everyone gridded in races still to come, was keyed by the driver's transponder. One request handed over a transponder beside a full name for the night's entire entry: the exact pairing the roster spends a check-in PIN to keep off the Wi-Fi. They now use the same anonymous id the roster uses. Two things follow from it: a phone following a driver forgets who it was following when HeatCaller is restarted, because the ids are new each time it starts; and the broadcast graphics page needs one refresh in OBS after this build, because it now tells a ghost from a real car by a mark on its row rather than by reading its id.

Holding an arrow key down now nudges the selection across the plan without eating what it travels over; it used to put the selection down on every press. <kbd>Esc</kbd>, or picking another tool, part-way through a drag now puts the pieces down where they are instead of leaving them stacked on top of whatever was underneath - invisible, counted twice by the parts list and the kit check, and saved that way. Typing the room's size is one <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>, and typing the size it already is adds nothing to undo. - **The Track Designer can pick part of a plan up and move it.** A new **Select** tool (<kbd>8</kbd>): drag a box round part of the plan and everything it covers comes up together — tiles, rails, turns, markings and racing-line points. Because the box snaps out to whole tiles, the rails round the edge of a block come with it rather than being left behind, which is what made sliding a layout across the room worth doing by hand before. Then drag it to move it, arrow keys to nudge it a tile at a time, **Turn** (<kbd>]</kbd>) to turn it a quarter clockwise, **Flip across** and **Flip down** to mirror it, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd> to copy it, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>D</kbd> to duplicate it one tile along, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>A</kbd> for the whole plan, <kbd>Delete</kbd> to take it off and <kbd>Esc</kbd> to drop it. Shift-drag adds to what's picked, or takes it back out. A turn or a mirror keeps the top-left corner of the box where it is, so turning a corner complex never slides it off the floor you had it lined up with — and a turn has to get three different things right at once, because a tile fills a cell, a rail lies along a seam and a turn rail sweeps a quadrant of a grid point. Every drag, nudge, turn and mirror is one press of <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>; picking something up isn't an edit, so it takes no undo step at all. The circuits are layouts *buildable from* each kit rather than copies of RCP's own — their published parts lists rule out the tight corners their photographs appear to show, so the presets use the big sweeps the boxes are actually built for. **The room it goes in** takes the width and depth of your hall in feet or meters, draws it round the plan, and says whether the layout fits turned either way, with how much room to spare or how much too big it is. Rails are also now drawn with the gray plastic bolts that hold them to the floor tiles, because that is what holds them on the real thing, and the plan says if a rail has no tile under it to bolt to.

- **The show can be scored with your own music.** The segments that open and close the evening — the cold open and the sign-off — can have a piece of music in front of the voice and another running underneath it. The opener lands, the bed comes up under it, and the first words arrive **over the opener's tail** rather than after it, so it sounds like a broadcast coming on air instead of a clip followed by an announcement. The bed sits under the whole read, drops a little further whenever the announcer is actually speaking, and when the read finishes it hands over to the piece's own fade and plays out. Your club's playlist goes quiet while it runs and comes back after; the crowd is left alone, because crowd noise under a broadcast open is the point. You choose the two files on **Voice → Commentary** and HeatCaller copies them into the club's data, so a backup carries them to the next laptop. The bed is looped — anything from about half a minute up works — and HeatCaller finds the fade on the end of it, keeps it back as the show's ending, and crossfades the loop's join, so you don't have to edit the file or find a loop point. **Play It** runs a real show with a test line over it, which is how the levels are meant to be set: by ear, not by numbers. It used to stay the old way round, putting every split out by twice the offset in sectors that still added up to the lap. The first sector record on a layout can't come from a split read early, and one early read no longer silences **Fastest sector** for the rest of the race. After a split loop is moved, **Record pace at the split** no longer compares with a record taken where the loop used to be. A split transponder claimed by two drivers shows as unknown at the split instead of quietly going to one of them, and undoing a driver edit or bringing back a deleted driver is refused when one of their split transponders has gone to someone else since. "Split loop not connected" stays on the Next Up bar for 15 seconds, then is left to the console and Mission Control, instead of turning the bar into a red alarm all race.
  - **Racing:** a jump start no longer sets the race's fastest lap. A car still waiting for its release, parked on the loop through a red flag, is no longer booked as a jump start when racing resumes. A transponder transferred to another driver mid-heat keeps its laps in the row it started in. A red flag in practice is refused, from the button, <kbd>F5</kbd> or the phone, which now says so: it used to stop practice laps counting with no green to clear it. A car on its warm-up laps no longer starts practice, which cancelled the warm-up and the heat waiting to stage. Undo of **Reset race** is refused once another race has started, so the next heat is no longer filed under the old round. Ghosts switched off mid-race now leave the track at once, and while ghosts are racing, switching one on or off adds or removes it straight away. They used to keep lapping, and being called, until the checkered. While real cars are on track, a ghost's lap is no longer shown as the session's fastest on the Big Screen, the Race Control board, the stat strip, **Practice** or the Live Results page.
  - **Heats and mains:** **Add Main** offers a third main as "C-Main" rather than "Main 3", which sat outside the bump-ups, and "A Final" is now read as a tiered main. A main added by hand goes in front of the higher mains still to run, so its finishers can bump up, and HeatCaller says when it's too late for that. Redoing the remaining heats keeps drivers who were added to a heat by hand from another class. With auto-advance following the class that was racing (the default), a race started with nothing picked in the Round box is filed against the heat whose roster holds the cars that were on track, not guessed from their classes. A driver added to a heat from another class keeps their "not in this class" mark when the heat is opened again. **Qualifying Standings** sorts the time, lap and heat columns by their figures, not as text, so "10.123s" no longer sorts above "9.500s".
  - **Results and records:** the event report has a **Status** column for DNF and DQ and lists the corrections made after the race, and the results card image shows DNF and DQ too. The card also says how many drivers it leaves out ("+3 more not shown, including 1 DQ"). When Edit Result takes off a phantom crossing that set the track record, the console now says the record still stands and to clear it on **Track Layouts**, and deleting the race day with records cleared now finds that record. Deleting a race day no longer clears a track record set tonight while the delete window was open, and undoing the delete makes the day current again only if it was current then. On **Pace**, a driver with fewer than three laps no longer tops **Best 3 in a row** or **Best 3 any**. A **Points** cell on the **Championship** page refuses anything but a whole number, instead of saving "1O" as 0. **Import from CSV** says which rows' points weren't numbers instead of dropping them. In the handicap suggestions, ticking a row after sorting the list credits that row's driver. Transferring a transponder that was timed as a car of its own in races already run now lists those races and asks before it moves, since they count for the new owner from then on. The **Transfer** window used to promise that races already run stay with whoever ran them. See [Transfer a transponder](race-day/drivers-and-classes.md#transfer-a-transponder). Duplicating a setup logged before chassis sheets keeps everything it says.
  - **Badges:** cars that never turned a lap no longer make up the field, so one car lapping a hand-released heat while the rest sat on the line no longer earns **Winner**, **On the Box** or **Full House**. A badge whose save failed is no longer announced, which used to call "First Timer" for the same drivers after every race.
  - **Announcer:** provisional pole goes to whoever tops the class's qualifying order (most laps then time, or the best run of laps in a row), checked on every crossing. It used to go to the quickest single lap, so a driver fifth on the board could be called P1 while the real leader never was. With **Announce ghosts like real drivers** off, a ghost on top no longer hides a real driver taking the lead, and is never named as a rival or chaser. In a most-laps qualifier, a lead change and the pole call no longer both speak for one pass, and qualifying heats no longer render a "go!" line for every car at the green. Round intros and the mechanic's interview no longer go out shouting: a lone word of four or more capitals, a hyphenated one ("HIGH-Octane") and a stretched one ("TIIIIIME") are calmed as well as runs of capitals, while short initials like "RC" stay. Team and sponsor names in a round intro keep their own spelling. With **Mention up to** set to 1, the recap no longer says there was no contest. A recap cut off by the next race is no longer offered to repeat on the **Announcer** page, and its writing stops so the next round's intro isn't held up. A recap spoken without being prepared ahead now shows on the Big Screen and the Announcer page's feed. A flag call made with the ElevenLabs quota spent now shows the quota as gone on the **Mixer** instead of filling the console with error text. When **Countdown started** is switched on on **Voice → Announcements**, the console says why it isn't spoken (the countdown is sound only, so the green isn't held up).

A sign-up approved on a club rental, picked or typed by its number, is lent the unit by name with no profile on it, which used to put every later borrower's laps under that driver's name. A rental that's already out or retired is refused before anything changes. A re-sent sign-up answer can no longer hand a phone another driver's name and check-in link. The warning about invites made on another PC stays while any of them is still out. Sign-up codes made while the settings couldn't be saved no longer turn "invalid" for good at the next launch. On the club website, standings and the results archive come back after the relay restarts instead of staying empty until the next save, a correction made while the relay couldn't be reached is sent later, and the archive no longer lists races run while publishing was off, or the club's whole history before anything was published. Re-sending one old result from the phone no longer pulls every race since into the archive, and the re-sent result can be found there.
  - **Your data:** a data file left empty (by a sync tool, say) is now reported in the console instead of loading silently as a fresh install. A check-in file in the wrong shape is kept aside before the first check-in of the night can write over every earlier race day's attendance. The crash journal is read again if something has it open at startup, and one that still can't be read is kept aside before the next race writes over it. A saved race whose journal couldn't be deleted is no longer offered back at the next launch, and stopping practice, or a race of nothing but ghosts finishing, no longer deletes the journal of a race still waiting to be saved. When recovering the club's locked keys is left until the next launch, HeatCaller no longer makes racer PINs in the meantime and saves the settings with only those, which lost the relay token, every PIN, the email and backup passwords and the QR check-in key. The list of backups shows when each was taken rather than when its file was last copied, and **Restore** names the backup and its date before it asks.

A laptop whose first start after the rename was a rehearsal now copies the club's real data, not an empty copy.

In **Belongs To**, typing in **Find** no longer resets the car and "it replaces" choices, which gave the transponder to the wrong car. - **The console is saved to a log file as it goes,** one file per run, in `%LocalAppData%\HeatCaller\Logs` (in a rehearsal, the `Logs` folder in `%LocalAppData%\HeatCaller Rehearsal`). The last 20 are kept. If the window stops responding for 10 seconds, the log says so, and a freeze dump is saved beside it once per run for working out why.
- **Starting a round from the Next Up bar always races that round.** A round built after the last race, such as a main seeded straight after qualifying, used to run as freeform: no round intro, and "No round selected" at the green. If a round can't be selected, the console says so.
- **Round intros keep to their length and the round's name.** An intro written in capitals is put back into normal case, a wrong heat number is replaced with the round's name, and an intro well past **Max length** (**Voice Announcements → Commentary**) is cut to whole sentences, or skipped if its first sentence alone is too long. The console says what was changed.
- **A car sitting on the loop isn't called as a jump start.** A car the decoder has seen on the loop since before the countdown is logged as sitting on the loop instead, and still starts the race with its start lap, like a car past the line. A car that goes on the lights, or is moved onto the loop during the countdown, is still a jump start.
- **The console says why a pass was too soon.** "Lap rejected" now gives the time since that car's previous pass, which is what the minimum lap time is checked against, with the lap time beside it.

## 0.46.0

A deep bug-hunt release. Most changes make race night behave the way you'd expect when two things happen at once.

**Starting races**

- **Stage over a running race asks first.** **Stage** or **Countdown** (or <kbd>F1</kbd>/<kbd>F2</kbd>) while a race is running shows **Race in progress**: use **Checkered** to end the race and keep it. If you go ahead, **Undo** brings the race back until the new race goes green. Starting a round from **Race Day → Program** asks "Abandon the running race?" before anything is announced. See [Staging over a running race](race-control/corrections-and-recovery.md#staging-over-a-running-race).
- **A late green is cancelled.** If you press **Reset**, **Stage**, **Red** or **Checkered** (or a demo is stopped) while the green is waiting for the countdown audio, the green isn't dropped. The log says "Green flag not dropped - ...".
- **A yellow or red stops the automatic start.** Thrown during staging or the countdown, it stops the automatic sequence and **Quick Race**. Press **Green (Start)** when ready, or **Stage** again. See [Running a race](race-control/running-a-race.md#start-the-race).
- **The phone remote says when a command wasn't sent**, instead of reporting success.
- **Race Control's track list** updates as soon as you add a layout, so races are saved with the right track.
- **A remembered round's format** is applied at startup.

**Practice and demos**

- **Races after practice** use their own lap or time limit and start type again.
- **Practice always uses a standard start**, and the car whose crossing auto-starts practice gets that lap counted.
- **Practice isn't crash-recovered.** Closing the app during practice stops it like **Stop** (and saves it if **Save automatically on stop** is ticked). At startup, gate auto-start waits until the unfinished-race question has been answered.
- **Start as Demo...** is refused while a race is staged, counting down or running. Practice and demos no longer mark a run sheet line as started.

**Crash recovery**

- **No need to remove the first lap after a restore.** Each car's first lap after the green is timed from its last crossing before the app closed: it counts as a lap, but never as a best lap or toward a clean run. See [Getting a race back](race-control/corrections-and-recovery.md#getting-a-race-back).
- **More comes back:** the start type, the grid, a staggered start's release schedule and clock, and jump starts.
- **Reset Round** clears the recovery copy too. A qualifying start corrects itself if the decoder was switched off and on.

**Results and scoring**

- **Time-credit handicaps can make up laps.** Each running driver's time is projected to the winner's lap count at their own pace before the credit comes off. Retired drivers rank behind those still running. See [How time credits are scored](race-control/practice-and-ghosts.md#how-time-credits-are-scored).
- **Alternate transponders in qualifying.** A driver who qualifies on their alternate transponder is ranked once, under their main one.
- **Event report follows the race day.** When a race day covers the chosen date, the report covers that race day's rounds, even past midnight, and its qualifying matches mains seeding. See [Event Report](race-control/results-and-championships.md#event-report).
- **Ghost cars** never get bump-up places and never appear in the event report's fastest laps or driver count.

**Race Day**

- **Renaming a class carries it everywhere:** drivers, rounds, championships, tech inspections, race days, handicap modes, run sheet lines and filters. One **Undo** reverses it. Class names must be unique, ignoring capitals. See [Rename a class](race-day/drivers-and-classes.md#rename-a-class).
- **Bump-ups can be skipped.** The **Apply bump-ups** prompt now has **Yes** (bump now), **No** (skip bump-ups for this main, undoable) and **Cancel** (decide later). A main with nobody to promote is marked done. See [Bump-ups](race-day/program-and-mains.md#bump-ups).
- **Move Drivers Between Heats** lists drivers missing from a qualifying round ("(not in Q2, Q3)") and won't put a driver in two heats of the same round.
- **Tech without a checklist** has a **Result:** list (**Passed**, **Passed with a note**, **FAILED**). Old inspections keep checks that were removed from the checklist. With no race day open, a pass counts only on the day it was recorded.
- **Undo is more careful.** Undoing a qualifying build or mains seeding keeps rounds already run and lists them. A deleted round comes back in its old place. Undo on the Calendar, run sheet and **Check Out Everyone** only reverses that one action and keeps later changes. Undo works after editing a tech inspection, rental unit or event name.
- **Run sheet:** a reminder countdown of 5 minutes or less doesn't announce "5 minutes", and late/early is right across midnight.
- **Qualifying Standings** opens the right driver on double-click after sorting. A championship whose class is gone shows "(missing class) *name*" and keeps it.

**Announcer and sound**

- The start horn, time-up buzzer and finish fanfare play along with their spoken calls.
- On a busy track, lap chimes more than about 3 seconds behind are skipped.
- **Last lap**, **Final minute** and **Track record** calls wait their turn instead of being skipped. Of two flag calls pressed close together, only the newer is spoken.
- Crowd and music duck under flag calls too. The **Ambience** fader changes loudness without changing the crowd's mood.
- **Voice Settings** and **Effects** no longer overwrite **Mixer** levels. The ambience layer editor's **Cancel** and **Undo** work, and closing it doesn't stop ambience.
- Race intros and recaps mention rivalries for drivers with an **Announcer name:**, and only numbers with a unit are read as times.

**Backups and files**

- **Automatic backups run in the background** and wait until no race or practice is running.
- **Restore** stops music, ambience and the I-Lap decoder first, keeps and lists locked music, audio and log files, and lists any data files it couldn't replace (naming the safety backup) before it restarts. See [Restoring a backup](admin/backups.md#restoring-a-backup).
- The music library survives a restore onto another PC or Windows account.
- **Track Layouts:** **Attach File...** and **Remove** take effect only on **Save**. **Remove** on the club logo only deletes HeatCaller's own logo files.

**Phones, Wi-Fi and online**

- **New staff PINs must be 6 to 8 digits.** A 4- or 5-digit staff PIN you already have keeps working, with a warning to change it. See [Online check-in](online/check-in.md).
- **Wi-Fi server:** wrong PINs are counted per device, IPv6 included. 50 wrong tries at a PIN in five minutes refuses that PIN for five minutes, except on devices that entered it in the last 12 hours. The server keeps running after a failed connection. See [Local Screens](screens/phone-and-web.md#security).
- **Racer PINs** that fall due wait until there's been no racing for 4 hours, so they don't change mid-meeting after midnight.
- **Check-in ordering** is right even if the laptop's clock is off.
- **Delete Everything Published** stops any upload in flight first.
- **Relay PIN lockouts grow:** 50 wrong PINs in 10 minutes closes PIN sign-in for the club for 10 minutes, then 30 minutes, 2 hours, 6 hours and 12 hours for repeat lockouts. Phones already signed in and QR check-ins keep working, and a new staff PIN reopens sign-in at once. The **Club Website** page shows when sign-in is locked. See [Lockouts](online/check-in.md#lockouts).
