# Part 2: Privacy notice

This notice explains what information about drivers HeatCaller handles when a club uses it, where that information goes, and how long it is kept.

**Who is responsible.** The club running the race night decides what it records and publishes, and is responsible for the information on its own computer. We (HeatCaller LLC, which makes HeatCaller) run the online relay, the HeatCaller voice and online AI commentary that clubs can choose to use, and handle driver information in them only to provide those services to the club.

**Children.** Many drivers are children. HeatCaller's defaults are set with that in mind: full names, rosters and photos stay off the internet unless the club turns them on, a junior's name is always shortened online, and a child's own words are never taken from their phone (see **Drivers' own crew and notes** below).

## What HeatCaller keeps on the club's computer

- Each driver's name, and optionally a spoken ("announcer") name, transponder numbers, class, car, photo and contact details the club types in, such as an email address.
- For a junior: their age band (under 13 or 13-17, never a date of birth), how their name may appear on a stream, and whether a parent agreed to their notes being said and to them appearing on a stream.
- Lap times, results, warnings and penalties, championship points and race history.
- Check-ins, race-day sign-ups and requests drivers send from their phones.
- Audio recordings of each race and, if the club turns it on, short highlight videos.
- The announcer's spoken lines, saved so they don't have to be made again.

Keys and PINs are stored encrypted, locked to the Windows account that saved them. Drivers' profiles and results on the club's computer are not encrypted, so keep the computer's own Windows sign-in private. When the club sets a backup password, each backup is encrypted with it, and HeatCaller will not copy backups to Google Drive, iCloud or OneDrive, or save them in one of their folders, without one.

## Drivers' own crew and notes

Only if the club turns on **Drivers can pick their own crew**.

**What is collected.** A driver, signed in with their personal PIN, can send: made-up names for their own Team Principal and Technical Lead; which of the club's voices each speaks in; a short about line; and up to three short notes in their own words - a goal for the night (80 characters), who they would like to thank (80) and their car's story (120). The club's desk can type the same things on the driver's profile. For juniors, see **Children's notes** below.

**How it is used.** Only to write and speak the announcer's commentary: the interviews with the driver's Team Principal and Technical Lead, and the race intros and recaps. A note is given to the commentary as something the driver said, at most once a night. Notes are never shown on the website, the results or the stream's graphics.

**Who sees or hears it.**

- **The club's desk** reads every name and note before it is used. Voice picks need no check and apply straight away.
- **Everyone at the track** hears what the announcer says over the PA.
- **The public, if the club streams.** What is said goes out on the club's stream (for example on YouTube), in its automatic captions and in any recording the club keeps. HeatCaller does not control the stream platform.
- **ElevenLabs** receives each spoken line to turn it into speech, if the club uses the HeatCaller voice or its own ElevenLabs account.
- **Anthropic** receives the notes as part of the facts the commentary is written from, if the club uses online AI commentary. Commentary written by the model built into HeatCaller, and the free Kokoro voice, run on the club's computer and send nothing.

**On the relay.** A driver's request passes through the relay to the club's computer and is deleted from the relay once the club's computer has collected it. A copy of the driver's current crew and notes is kept on the relay so that their own phone, signed in with their personal PIN, can show it. That copy stays until the club publishes again without it, forgets the driver, or uses **Delete Everything Published**.

**Children's notes.**

- **Desk only:** a junior's notes and about line are only ever typed at the club's desk, with a parent or guardian there. A junior's phone can only send the name the announcer calls them, and, for a junior aged 13-17, crew names for the desk to check. An under-13's crew names are typed at the desk.
- **Consent:** a junior's notes and about line are only spoken once the club has recorded on their profile that a parent or guardian agreed.
- **Streams:** on a night the club streams, a junior's notes and about line are not spoken unless the club has chosen to allow it.
- **No conditions:** a junior's crew names and voice picks are not needed to race, and nobody has to give notes to take part.
- **Parents' rights:** a parent or guardian can ask the club to see their child's information, change or delete it, or stop any more being collected. They can also email us about anything held on the relay.

## Where it goes

**The club's Wi-Fi.** If the club starts the local screens server, devices on the venue's Wi-Fi can see live results and use check-in. Nothing goes over the internet for this.

**The online relay**, only if the club sets up online publishing. The relay is hosted by Fly.io in the United States. It receives:

- Results, standings and the live race. Names are shortened to a first name and an initial by default ("Billy S"); the club can turn that off for adults, but a junior's name is always shortened. Pages that name a junior ask search engines not to list them.
- The roster and heat line-ups, only if the club turns them on.
- Driver photos, only in the online check-in list, only if the club turns them on, and only visible to someone with a check-in code or PIN.
- Highlight videos for the top three in a race.
- Check-ins, sign-ups and requests drivers make from their phones.
- Follows and phone notifications. Anyone can follow an adult driver by name. A junior can only be followed through a family link made from their own phone.

Transponder numbers and email addresses are never published.

**Streams.** Overlays, captions and listen-along show or say a junior's name only as their **On stream** setting allows: shortened, or not at all.

The relay also sees the internet address of every phone or computer that opens the club's pages. It holds it in memory for ten minutes at most, only to limit PIN guessing and floods of requests, and does not write it down. The pages send a few anonymous daily counts of how they are used (for example how often a phone lost its signal), with no driver, phone or address in them, kept 90 days. What the pages keep in the browser is set out in the cookie policy (Part 3).

**ElevenLabs** (a speech service), only if the club uses the HeatCaller voice or its own ElevenLabs account. **Each announcer line is sent to ElevenLabs to be turned into speech, and a line that names a driver carries that driver's name.** Names are sent in the short form ("Billy S") while **Shorten published names** is on, unless the club ticks **Say full names aloud with the online voices**. While **Ask ElevenLabs not to keep our lines** is on (it is by default), each request asks ElevenLabs not to log or keep the line, where ElevenLabs offers that on the account in use. With **Offline voices for juniors** on, a line that names a junior is written and spoken on the club's computer instead, and sent to neither ElevenLabs nor Anthropic. The free Kokoro voice runs on the club's computer and sends nothing anywhere.

**Anthropic** (an AI model provider), only if the club uses online AI commentary. The race facts the commentary is written from, including drivers' names, positions and lap times, are sent through the relay to Anthropic's model. We use Anthropic's commercial service, whose terms say it does not train its models on what we send. Commentary written by the model built into HeatCaller, on the club's computer, sends nothing.

**Neither we nor HeatCaller train AI models** on drivers' information.

**Updates.** HeatCaller checks GitHub for new versions. This sends no driver information.

**Usage totals.** Only if the club uses the online relay, and only while **Send HeatCaller anonymous usage totals** on **Backup & Restore** is ticked. It is ticked to begin with only on a computer whose Windows region is the United States (or a US territory). Everywhere else it starts unticked, and nothing is sent unless the club ticks it. Once a day, HeatCaller sends us, through the club's relay, a few totals for each day it was used: which of its pages and tabs were opened, how many times, and for how long; the HeatCaller version; and whether a race day was open. We use them only to decide what to improve in HeatCaller. They carry the club's relay name, because they go with the club's relay sign-in, but never a driver's name or anything else about a driver, nothing typed into HeatCaller, no results, no keys, and not the order in which anything was done. Unticking the box stops them from the next day; nothing is sent while the relay is off.

**Email.** Check-in PIN emails and drivers' nights by email are sent from the club's own mailbox, not by us.

We do not sell driver information or use it for advertising.

## How long it is kept

- **On the club's computer:** until the club deletes it, or forgets the driver. Race recordings and highlight videos are kept for the number of race nights the club chooses on **Club Website > Recording**.
- **Freeze reports** (saved if HeatCaller stops responding): 14 days, then deleted.
- **Usage totals on the relay:** 13 months, then deleted, and deleted with the rest of a club's relay data when the club leaves.
- **Podium highlight videos on the relay:** one week, then deleted.
- **Online check-ins on the relay:** ignored after 12 hours.
- **Online sign-ups on the relay:** 14 days. The club's own record stays on its computer.
- **Spoken announcer lines on the relay:** a line that names a driver is kept for that club alone and never shared with another club.
- **Drivers' crew requests on the relay:** until the club's computer collects them, then deleted.
- **Follows and phone notifications:** deleted after 180 days without use.
- **A junior's sign-in on a phone:** ends after 30 days; they sign in again with their PIN.
- **A driver's goal for the night:** used on that night only (or, sent after the night's last race, the next race night within eight days), then never again. It stays on the profile until it is replaced or deleted.
- **Other crew names, notes and about lines:** until the driver or the club changes or deletes them, or the driver is forgotten.
- **Old results:** a club can choose to take results older than a set number of years off the relay (**Unpublish results older than**). This is off unless the club turns it on.
- **Everything else published to the relay:** until the club publishes again, forgets the driver, or uses **Delete Everything Published**.

## Choices a club and a driver have

- **Shorten published names** is on by default, and also applies to the names sent to the online voices. Juniors' names are shortened online whatever it is set to.
- A club can keep the roster, photos and full names off the internet, publish nothing at all, or use **Delete Everything Published** to erase everything it has on the relay at once.
- A club can stop sending usage totals, or start them outside the United States, with **Send HeatCaller anonymous usage totals** on **Backup & Restore**.
- Each driver's profile has **Online privacy** choices: leave their photo out, no podium videos of them, and initials only. A junior's profile also has **On stream**.
- A driver can clear any of their crew names or notes from their phone, and the club can delete them on the driver's profile. A club can turn off **Drivers can pick their own crew** at any time; their crews are then not heard.
- **A copy of a driver's information:** **Export** on the driver's menu in Driver Hub saves everything HeatCaller holds about them in one file, for the club to give the driver or their parent.
- **Removing a driver:** **Forget** on the driver's menu in Driver Hub deletes what HeatCaller holds about them on the club's computer, and their copies on the relay. Their past results stay, shown as "Removed driver", so other drivers' places and points don't change.
- A driver, or a parent or guardian, can ask the club for a copy of their information or for it to be removed. For anything on the relay or in the HeatCaller voice, they or the club can also email us.

# Part 3: Cookies on your club's pages

Your club's online pages (the pages the relay serves for your club) keep a small amount in the browsers that open them: one cookie for the colour theme a driver picks, notes in the browser's own storage for signing in and for choices drivers make, and copies of the club's public pages for when the signal drops. They also send us a few anonymous counts of how the pages are used. They carry no advertising and do not track anyone across other websites.

**The cookie policy.** What the pages keep, why, for how long, and what drivers can do about it is set out in the cookie policy at /c/<your club>/cookies on the relay, linked as **Cookies** at the foot of every one of your club's pages. It is written for your drivers, their parents and anyone else who opens the pages. We keep it up to date as the pages change and date every change on it. Changing it does not change these terms, and HeatCaller will not ask you to accept them again for it. You can also ask us for a copy at any time.

**HeatCaller's part.** We decide what the pages keep in a browser, and we are responsible for the cookie policy being accurate. We will not make the pages keep anything new in a browser without first adding it to the cookie policy.

**Your part.**

- Do not tell your drivers or visitors anything about the pages that contradicts the cookie policy. When you tell drivers about the online pages, for example in a sign-up email or at the desk, point them to it.
- Links you add to the pages (your website, social media, your stream) lead to other sites with their own cookies. If you show your own stream or anything else from another site on the pages, you are responsible for telling your drivers about it as the law where you race requires.
- We built the pages to keep only what they need for the things drivers ask them to do, so that, as far as we know, they need no consent banner. If the law where you race requires a notice or consent beyond the cookie policy, tell us and we will work with you on it. Until we have, you are responsible for any notice or consent that law requires you to give your own drivers.
- Where your drivers are children, tell their parents or guardians that the cookie policy exists and where to find it.
